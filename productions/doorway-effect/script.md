# Why You Forget Why You Walked Into a Room
mood: curious

- You walk into a room and instantly forget why. Scientists think the doorway did it.
- Meet Milo. He needs his keys.
- He heads to the kitchen to grab them.
- milo: Wait... why am I in here?
- He checks the fridge, a drawer, even the toaster. The thought is just gone.
- Psychologist Gabriel Radvansky put this to the test.
- People carried objects through rooms, then were quizzed on what they were carrying.
- Those who walked through a doorway forgot more than those who walked the same distance in one room.
- milo: Wait, a door made me forget?
- You see, your brain doesn't record life as one long video. It cuts it into scenes.
- Psychologists call each cut an event boundary, and a doorway is a big one.
- Walk through it, and your brain files the old scene away to make room for the new one.
- Milo's plan for the keys got filed with the sofa.
- It doesn't happen every time. Later studies found it mostly when people's minds were busy.
- And going back can help, because the old room is full of cues.
- So Milo walks back to the sofa... and remembers.
- He grabs his keys, heads out the door... and forgets where he was going.
