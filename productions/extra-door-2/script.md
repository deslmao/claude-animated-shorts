## Answers to your questions

### 1. Are both Juno messages readable and believable as phone notifications?
**Yes**, with the first one tightened:
- **"Left work early. I'm outside, open the door?"** (44 characters, about 3 short lines in a bubble).
  - It answers the obvious doubt before the viewer has it: the chat thread directly above still shows "No, I'm still
    at work. Why?" from a few minutes ago.
  - The comma version reads like a real text, not a sentence.
- **"Who's that in your upstairs window?"** (35 characters, 2 lines) needs no context. It is the kind of thing someone
  types fast from a driveway.

**Why chat bubbles, not lock-screen banners.** Milo still has the chat open from Part 1, and with the app open, a
phone shows a new message as a bubble in the thread, plus the buzz. That also keeps Part 1's exchange on screen as
context. The phone clock moves on a minute or two from Part 1's 11:47.

**Readability.** Each insert holds about 2.8 s. That is about 16 and 12 characters a second, an easy read, and longer
than Part 1's 2 s hold. The bubbles use Part 1's type size, which measured about 50 px capitals at 1080×1920, so it
reads without zooming. Only the newest bubble needs to be large in frame; the older ones sit higher, partly cropped.
I'll measure it at the stills checkpoint, as in Part 1.

### 2. Does the script establish that the upstairs voice and the person outside can't both be trusted?
**In v1, only half way.** The voice was clearly wrong (it comes from where the impossible door is). The person outside
relied on viewers remembering Part 1's knock rhythm and noticing the "still at work" timing.

v2 gives each side one reason to believe and one reason to doubt, and shows the doubt rather than saying it:

| | Reason to believe | Reason to doubt |
|---|---|---|
| **Upstairs** | It's Juno's voice, and it warns him. | It speaks from where the impossible door now stands; it's the voice that said "Don't text her". |
| **Outside** | It's Juno's real number in the real chat, and she warns him about someone upstairs. | Minutes ago she was "still at work"; the knocks are exactly the old door's knocks; and **no one's shadow is behind the glass** being knocked on. |

"…which one is her?" then says out loud what the viewer is already thinking. Both votes stay genuinely risky.

### 3. Is there a stronger way to make the repeated photograph understandable without adding runtime?
**Yes: give it a mark that only this photograph can have, and make it Milo's own doing.** Three cues:
1. **His foot cracks the glass across Juno's face** in shot 5. A copy could look the same, but it couldn't carry the
   crack he just made.
2. **The second appearance is an exact match cut** of the first framing, the most direct way film says "this again".
   He stops dead on it, so the viewer has a beat to register it.
3. **When he looks back, the steps are bare.** The photo was never on the stairs at all.

Runtime is about the same as v1: the deleted mid-sequence count paid for the longer hold on the second photo. The
crack across Juno's face also quietly feeds the ending question, "which one is her?"
