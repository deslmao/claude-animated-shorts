**Status:** design for owner review. Nothing has been built, generated or rendered yet.
**Narration:** the locked approved-timing candidate, 65.33 s, 14 lines, unchanged. The timestamps below come from its

## Visual identity: "Race Day, 1904"
This is a fresh look for this story, not borrowed from Tickle or any other video.
- **World:** one long, winding dirt road through hot Missouri farmland: rolling hills, rail fences, a cornfield, an
  orchard bend, a lone stone well. It is a single continuous course, so every scene feels like a point further along the
  same race.
- **Light:** a hot, dusty afternoon (warm "golden" light, low haze). It contrasts with the cool modern street used for
  the "Today" beat.
- **Chapter markers are in-world:** wooden mile-marker signs along the road ("MILE 9", "WATER MILE 12", "MILE 20") tell
  the viewer where they are without narration.
- **The race board:** a chalk board at the finish is the story's scoreboard. It counts the starters ("STARTERS: 32"),
  shows the times and places, the ban, the "14 / 32" finishers, and finally the ban being cut.
- **Recurring motif, dust.** Cars kick it up, runners cough through it, and the last image is clean dust from Lorz's own
  feet.
- **Camera grammar:**
  - swoops and whips *along the road* carry the race's momentum between scenes;
  - macro inserts land on each bizarre object (headlamp, vial, egg, flask, rotten apple, shoes);
  - POV shots put the viewer in the moment: Lorz's seat in the car, Carvajal looking up at the apples, a dog's-eye chase;
  - low hero angles are for finishes, a high angle is for the officials' judgement.
- **Every event gets a clear action** (hopping into the car, the breakdown, the egg being poured, the nap, the chase). No
  one stands still while the narrator explains.

## Accuracy guard (visual choices that don't add claims)
- No race bib numbers, no starting pistol, no names on screen beyond what the narration says.
- Hicks is supported by his handlers, never carried (accounts differ).
- "Rat poison" is shown by Hicks's reaction, not by a rat-poison label on what he was given.
- The organizer's motive is never written on screen (the script says "reportedly").
- The car breaks down at a "MILE 20" sign: nine miles plus the next eleven, the script's own figures.
- No car appears at the Boston finish. "On foot" is shown with Lorz's feet alone, and the contrast is carried by the
  edit (the hook's car, the breakdown, the feet).
- Garcia collapses and is helped; nothing implies he died.
- "BANNED 6 MONTHS" matches the AAU's commutation (Wikipedia, research fact 21).

## Music and sound plan
- **Music:** the approved "playful" mood throughout, with a brief "curious" colour under the organizer (scene 3) and
  the "You see" explanation (scene 9), then back to playful for the ending.
  - It stops dead on "It was a joke!" and the officials' silent beat.
  - It drops out in the 0.69 s pause before "On foot.".
  - The existing music **button** lands on "foot.".
- **Ambience:** countryside (birds, breeze) on the road; crowd murmur at the finish; city traffic for "Today".
- **Effects already in the library:** footsteps on dirt, poof, whoosh, impact, gasp, applause, snore, clink, boing,
  twinkle, oof, stumble, drip, crowd murmur.
- **New effects needed (synthesised for free, like the others):** car engine putter, bulb-horn honk, backfire bang
  with steam hiss, dog bark, egg crack.
