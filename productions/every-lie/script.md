## Readability rules (v2)
- **The first sign is unmistakable.** It appears alone, big, top-centre over Milo, held about 0.8 s before anything
  stacks. Its text is the exact words he just said ("NEVER TOLD A LIE"), so "the lie appears above your head" needs no
  explanation. The red **LIE** stamp is the biggest element on it.
- **At most two signs on screen at once,** except the deliberate stacking gag in scene 1. Signs are **three words or
  fewer**. A footnote appears only after the main sign has been readable for about 0.5 s.
- **Captions stay off the signs** (the engine already places captions clear of props). Lines over a sign are kept
  short.

## Why it works
- **Hook in 1 second.** A sincere close-up, an obviously untrue line, and the joke is visible before the sentence
  ends. The tower through the ceiling tops it.
- **The rule is shown, not explained.** Scene 1 already teaches it. The one narrator line in scene 2 just names what
  you saw over three instant gags.
- **The escalation ladder:**
  1. a personal lie;
  2. the world's lies;
  3. a mutual lie (Dad and Milo, each caught);
  4. a lie with a footnote that roasts him;
  5. a callback that roasts the dog;
  6. Milo fighting the system;
  7. a lie the crowd celebrates anyway (the 67);
  8. the vow, the biggest lie;
  9. the narrator caught lying.
- **The twist breaks the fourth wall.** The narrator, the one voice you trust, gets a LIE sign the size of the
  screen. "He did not." gets no sign, so the silence is the punchline.
- **The memes work without the context.** "67" plays as Milo's bad test score plus a class chant with a funny hand
  gesture. If you know the meme it's a bonus; if you don't, it's still a crowd going wild over a lie. The phonk is
  the "tiki tiki" energy: hard cowbell stabs on every sign slam.
- **Milo is the engine:** angelic, then crunching charcoal, then a bucket on his head, cool, sincere and smug. Every
  scene is his decision going wrong.

## Comedy and sound plan
**Music:** an original phonk bed. It's synthesised in-house; nothing licensed or sampled. It has a cowbell melody,
808 slides and a drift-style 4-on-the-floor beat.
- It hits hard on the hook and the 67 drop.
- It cuts to dead silence for the two awkward beats: the breakfast stare, and the moment after the giant LIE.
- It plays calm and wholesome (an existing "warm" mood) under the fake moral, so the slam lands harder.

**Sound effects:**

| Moment | Sound |
|---|---|
| Every LIE sign | A neon buzz plus a heavy stamp thud (vine-boom weight), and a cowbell hit in time with the beat. |
| Stacking signs | Bonk, bonk, bonk, rising in pitch. |
| Through the ceiling | A crack and a plaster puff (existing crack plus poof). |
| Charcoal pancake | A crunch (existing thud plus crunch) and a tiny violin squeak on the tears. |
| Awkward stare | The music cuts; crickets. |
| "Ooooh!" | Crowd (existing crowd sound). |
| "SIX SEVEN" | A chant (one voice layered) plus a phonk drop. |
| Facepalm | A slap. |
| Whips and crash zooms | Whoosh (existing). |
| The final giant LIE | The biggest stamp, a bass drop, then total silence. |
| "…He did not." | A single soft "ding" (truth). |

**Comedy timing:**
- Each sign lands on the last stressed word: the punchline arrives with the stamp, never after it.
- Reactions hold for 0.4–0.8 s: long enough to read the sign, short enough to stay chaotic.
- The two silences (the breakfast stare and the giant LIE) are the only places the film stops moving.

## Camera language
- **Crash zooms (swoops)** on every slam.
- **Whip pans** between Dad and Milo, and to the window dog.
- **A low hero shot** of the teacher.
- **POV shots:** Milo looking up at his own sign; Dad looking at Milo's sign.
- **Inserts:** the pancake box, the test paper ("12"), the dog's mouth full of paper.
- **Quick-cut montages:** the world gags, hiding, chanting.
- **One big crane-up** for the vow sign through the roof.
- **No static wide holds.**
