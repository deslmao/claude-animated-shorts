# Independent evaluation: One Set of Footprints

## Criteria
### clarity
- result: weak
- beats: 2, 6, 8, 13
- evidence: Beat 2 has "you" refuse Ivy ("No woods.") and click off her light, which reads as a parent. Only in beat 8 do we hear "My sister's gone. She's seven." No adult appears anywhere, at 2 a.m., in a house with a seven-year-old. Beat 6 puts two unexplained things in one shot: the empty slot where your boots were, and "Ivy's tiny boots, soaked and steaming" by the radiator. Beat 11 shows Ivy wearing *your* boots, so her own boots being wet is never explained. Beat 13 shows "two trails, one big and one small", but Ivy is in your huge boots and nothing tells us what you are wearing.
- problem: A first-time viewer can't say who "you" is (parent or sibling? where are the parents?) until two-thirds of the way through. The wet small boots point at Ivy but make no sense once we learn she left in your boots, so a careful viewer either gets confused or feels cheated. The closing image doesn't match what we saw.
- fix: Add one line in beat 1 or 3, e.g. "Mum's on a night shift. You're in charge." Replace the soaked boots with Ivy's small boots *dry, still on the rack* beside your empty slot: whoever left took the big boots on purpose. In beat 9, have you pull on Mum's wellies and grab Ivy's small boots on the way out, then in beat 12 swap her into them, so "one big and one small" is true.

### viewer participation
- result: weak
- beats: 2, 5, 6, 7, 9
- evidence: The clues are genuinely checkable and fair: one trail and "None go back" (1), the dry doormat next to a snow-crusted step (5), the star in the print against your missing star-soled boots (3, 6), the short stride (7), and the silent backward step where "your toe sinks deep, just like hers" (9). The two most important deductions are spoken for the viewer, though. "Ivy's asleep. At least, that's what it looks like." (2) hands over the theory before any evidence exists, and "The prints came in. But your boots went out." (7) states the solution aloud.
- problem: The viewer is pushed to "it's Ivy" by narration at beat 2 rather than reaching it, and at beat 7 the narrator does the last step for them. What could be a satisfying "I got it before the narrator" moment turns into confirming what they were told. Muted viewers lose nothing here; sound-on viewers lose the puzzle.
- fix: Cut "At least, that's what it looks like." In beat 7, hold the split frame silently for about a second and a half before any line, or replace the line with a question that leaves the inference to the viewer ("Who walks out of a house and leaves tracks coming in?"). Leave beat 9's silent proof as it is: it's the best participation moment in the film.

## Stress tests
### plot hole
- applies: yes, a mystery depends on its logic holding up
- result: weak
- beats: 1, 2, 6, 8, 11, 13
- finding: (a) Where are the adults? A child is alone with an older sibling at 2 a.m., and nothing says why. This is the likeliest top comment ("where are the parents??"). (b) Why are Ivy's own boots soaked and steaming if she left in yours? No beat explains an earlier trip. (c) Ending: Ivy is in your huge boots, so the "small" trail home can't be hers unless she changed boots. (d) What are you wearing on your feet when you run into the snow (9–10), since your boots are gone? (e) Why do you wake at 2 a.m.? Easily covered by the sound of the unlatched door banging in the wind. (f) Why did Ivy need your boots? It's never said. It reads as hiding a child's prints, but her short stride undoes it, which is fine (she's seven) if the viewer gets the motive; one word in beat 11 would do it ("…in big feet, so nobody would find him"). The rest holds: the dispatcher's 40-minute delay explains why you go yourself, and the flurry explains why the trail can't simply be followed. Top-comment probe: "Where are the parents?", then "Why were her boots wet if she wore yours?", then possibly "Don't touch wild foxes", which beat 13's rescue van answers.

### giveaway
- applies: yes
- result: weak
- beats: 1, 2
- finding: "Ivy's asleep. At least, that's what it looks like." tells the viewer the bed is a decoy before a single clue has been shown. Coming straight after the fox plea and "No woods", in a story whose first line says the prints "come out of the woods", an attentive viewer solves it at second 10, and the intruder theory (open door, missing tuna, THUMP) never gets to scare them. The drawings and the face-down book are good plants. The narrator's line is an announcement. Cut the line. Also consider moving the spoken plea flash ("Please, he's hurt!" / "No woods.") to beat 8, when the pillows and the book page are revealed, and keep only the wall drawings and the book in beat 2. The intruder reading then survives until beat 5, as it should.

### dilemma balance
- applies: yes, no on-screen choice, but the mystery runs on two competing explanations (someone came in vs. someone went out)
- result: weak
- beats: 1–7
- finding: Intruder (A): the trail toward the door and "None go back" (1), the unlocked door (3), the missing tuna and the THUMP (4).
