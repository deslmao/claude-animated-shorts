**Milo and Juno:** they live together. In Part 1 he texted "are you home?" and she replied "No, I'm still at
work. Why?".

## The door's rules: two of them, both seen before the climax
1. **It moves only while something is watching it.**
   - **Watched,** by eyes or a camera lens, it grows toward you: wood spreads and a handle pushes through.
   - **Unwatched,** it can't move. It shrinks back into its photo.
2. **It travels between copies of one photo: Milo and Juno's.**
   - **The copies:** the framed print on Milo's hallway nail, the print on Juno's lab desk, and the same picture
     used as Juno's contact photo on Milo's phone.
   - **What it does:** it steps out of whichever copy is being watched, and it speaks in the voices of the people
     in the photo.
   - **Why removing a photo doesn't help:** pulling a photo away doesn't stop a door that is already out. Only
     putting it out of sight works.

The sound backs this up: whenever the door is being watched, the music drops to a low drone.

## How the five problems are solved
1. **A consistent watching rule:**
   - **One rule:** the door moves only while it is watched; unwatched, it shrinks back into its photo.
   - **Shown four times before it matters:** Part 1's handle grows while Milo stares; it stops when he looks away
     at the top of the stairs (scene 4); the phone handle stops when he slams it face down (scene 8); the monitor
     clip shows it shrinking when the feed drops (scene 12).
   - **Why the camera matters:** Juno says it on the monitor ("Eyes or cameras."). When they turn their backs, the
     door keeps moving (scene 19), because something is still watching it. Juno spots the camera, and Milo states the
     rule in seven words as she kills the power.
   - **The drone:** the music gives way to a drone whenever it is watched, so the audience hears the rule too.
2. **The link between the photographs:**
   - **There is one photo with three copies:** Milo's hallway print, Juno's desk print and Juno's contact photo on
     Milo's phone.
   - **The monitor shows the exact path:** the door learned them from the desk photo it faced, and at 21:14 it
     folded into that photo and came out of Milo's copy. A match cut shows Milo's photo sliding off the nail,
     which explains Part 1's photo on the floor.
   - **Where it appears from then on:** only where a copy of that photo is watched: his phone screen, then her desk.
3. **A shorter monitor reveal:** it was 9 s and five lines of backstory. It is now three wordless clips of about
   1.8 s each (the door facing the photo, the feed drop and the shrink, the escape into the photo), with three short
   lines (20 words). The "burned house" backstory is cut: the clips show everything the climax needs.
4. **The street gets a real obstacle:** the door comes *through his phone*. The contact photo turns to wood and a
   tiny handle pushes out of the glass. He has to choose: he slams the phone face down into a flooded gutter and
   abandons it. It is physical, it is memorable, and it applies the rule. It also explains why he arrives at the lab
   with no phone, and why Juno insists he prove who he is.
5. **A stronger final sting:**
   - **The clue first:** the home camera's motion alert, whose thumbnail shows the *other* copy of the photo lying
     face up in the hallway, being watched by that camera.
   - **The audience works it out:** they connect "watched copy" to "it got out again" a beat before the text from
     Milo's number lands: "I'm home. Come let me in."
   - **The ending:** a hard cut. The episode is complete (the lab threat is sealed), and the sting ends on the rule
     itself, with no Part 3 needed.

**Preserved:**
