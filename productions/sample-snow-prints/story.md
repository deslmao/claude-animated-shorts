# One Set of Footprints
spec: 2
format: immersive+mystery
why: The viewer wakes into an impossible clue (footprints that come in and never leave) and investigates alongside the protagonist; two explanations (an intruder inside vs something else) gain visible evidence; the decisive clue (your missing boots) lets the viewer solve it a beat early; and the reveal recontextualises every earlier image, ending with a warm payoff.
length: 65
voice: georgeHushed
voices: you=liam, ivy=jessica
mood: tense
factual: no

## Premise
- question: Footprints come out of the woods to your door and none go back. Who came in, and where are they now?
- protagonist: you (the viewer, as the older sibling), home alone at night with your little sister Ivy
- want: to keep Ivy safe while Mum is at work
- fear: that someone is inside the house with you both
- protects: Ivy
- decision: to stop searching the house and follow the tracks into the woods before the snow fills them
- loses: Ivy, if you read the clues wrong; a flurry is starting and will erase the only trail
- obstacle: the evidence points to an intruder inside; the truth is hidden in what's missing
- rule: none
- conflict: A, someone came out of the woods and is hiding in the house; B, nobody came in at all: the prints were made by someone leaving
- action: none

## Spine
depth: deep

### Constraints
- The first snow stopped at midnight. A short flurry starts just after 2 a.m., and it fills tracks within minutes.
- Mum is on a night shift at the hospital. Her phone is in her locker, so calls go to voicemail.
- The roads are snowed in, so a police car is about forty minutes away.
- Only one pair of boots in the house has a star on the sole: yours.
- Walking backwards, people take short steps and press down on their toes; the toe of each print sinks deeper than the heel.
- The woods are forbidden: there's a wild fox, and you said "No woods" when Ivy begged to help it.
- Ivy is seven, clever and stubborn. Her detective book has a page on making tracks point the wrong way.

| 2 | LOST DOG posters on every lamppost, for a dog you've never seen, with your address as the owner's | take the dog in when it turns up at your door | the dog belonged to the house's previous family, who moved and left it behind | a dilemma: keep the dog, or drive it to a family who abandoned it |
| 3 | your new house's old baby monitor picks up a child counting down every night | knock on the neighbour's door instead of calling the police | the sound comes through the shared wall: a lonely boy is counting down to his dad's call from overseas | you and the boy count down together, and the call comes |
- chosen: 1. It gives the clearest physical evidence a viewer can solve (the boots), the strongest recontextualisation (every early image changes meaning), real time pressure (the flurry), and a payoff that resolves the fear and changes the protagonist (from "No woods" to helping).

## Writer notes
The spine was revised once after the phase-1 evaluation; the lines and storyboard were written from the revised spine. An open doubt: whether "she's seven" plus the empty bed is too frightening for the tone. It resolves within about 25 seconds.

## Revision
**One revision, at spine level, after the phase-1 evaluation:**
- **The 999 call is visible** (roads snowed in, forty minutes out), and the foot test is folded into the chase.
- **The toe depth is matched, not the stride:** the short stride is now a fair clue that it's a child.
- **The early clues are less of a giveaway:** the book's title is unreadable in E2 and the lump isn't held on.
- **The intruder theory gets a misleading clue:** the open pantry and the missing tuna.
- **The boot plant is clear:** your boots on the rack in the 10:02 flash, and Ivy's soaked boots by the radiator.
- **Ivy gets a bedtime plea and a child's motive:** hiding the fox, not faking a burglar.
- **A tuna tin instead of cat food**, a short flurry instead of a jump to dawn, and a wildlife rescue for the fox.
