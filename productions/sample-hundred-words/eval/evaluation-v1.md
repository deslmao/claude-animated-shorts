## Criteria
### clarity
- result: weak
- beats: 1, 3, 4, 7, 8
- evidence: Beat 1 sets the rule clearly ("Every word you say ticks the counter on your wrist"), and beats 3-4 make the family readable (Milo calls Gus "Grandpa", and Mia is a little girl at home whose hand is placed in his). But beat 8 hangs the climax on "a small yellow coat", and no earlier beat shows Mia wearing a yellow coat (beats 3, 4 and 5 never mention it). Beat 7 also depends on a rule that was never shown: at 0 words "Milo's mouth opens with nothing", so you apparently go physically mute, but beats 1-2 only show the counter ticking down, never what happens at zero.
- problem: A cold viewer at beat 8 sees a yellow coat in a crowd and has to guess that it's Mia, at the moment the danger should land instantly. At beat 7 they can't tell whether Milo can't speak or is choosing not to break the rule, so the guard scene reads as "why doesn't he just say her name?" instead of helplessness.
- fix: Put Mia in a bright yellow coat in beat 4 (the hands close-up shows the yellow sleeve) and again in beat 5 at the edge of the stage. In beat 1 or 2, add a one-second gag of someone at 0 whose mouth moves with no sound, so the rule "zero means silence" is known before Milo hits it.

### setup and payoff
- result: weak
- beats: 3, 4, 5, 9, 10, 11
- evidence: The whistle setups pay off well. "From your belly, not your lips" is planted in beat 3 and used in beat 9, and Mia's whistle-back is planted in beat 3 and used in beat 10. Neither is over-announced. But the most prominent setup in the script, "Keep ten words for her. Promise." / "Promise." (beat 4), has no payoff. Milo breaks it in beat 5, and the story never comes back to it. Beat 11 instead says "Milo still had no words. He didn't need them." Gus "taps Milo's counter, then his own heart", which a viewer can read as forgiveness, as a rebuke, or as "words don't matter".
- problem: The ending's moral ("he didn't need words") cuts against the story's own setup, which was that words are precious and should be saved for the people you love. The broken promise is the emotional core of the middle, so leaving it unresolved makes the ending feel slightly off-topic, and lets Milo off without any sign that he's changed.
- fix: Pay off the promise in beat 11, or in a 2-second tag. For example, the next morning his counter resets to 100 and Milo taps it, turns to Mia and silently mouths/holds up ten fingers: the ten are hers now. Or cut "He didn't need them" and let Gus's tap on the counter be met by Milo's guilty look before the whistle and the heart tap, so the gesture clearly reads "you broke it, and you made it right."

## Stress tests
### plot hole
- applies: yes, the story turns on Milo being unable to get help
- result: weak
- beats: 2, 7, 8
- finding: Three questions a viewer will ask. (1) Why can't Milo write her name for the guard? The world bans spoken words, and beat 2's "Menus are pictures" suggests writing may also be restricted, but this is never established. Beat 7 should close it off, for example by having the guard hold out his own counter or a "no paper" gag, or by having Milo pat his empty pockets. (2) Why can't he shout or scream a non-word? Beat 9 partly answers this, since only the family whistle would mean something to Mia, but that logic isn't stated. It's fine as long as the whistle is clearly the family call (it is, from beat 3). (3) Why doesn't Mia, who has words, call out for him? Beat 8 shows her drifting calmly "with the crowd", so she doesn't know she's lost. That's acceptable if the animation shows her contented, not searching. The first question is the real hole. The others hold up.
### consistency
- applies: yes, the premise is literal word counting, so viewers will count
- result: weak
- beats: 4, 5, 7
- finding: (1) Beat 7: the woman's counter shows 2, yet she says "Two left. Sorry. For my son.", which is five words. Either her counter is wrong or she spent five words to protect two, which undercuts the rule in the one beat where the rule must feel absolute. (2) Beat 5: the joke "Why did the clown quit? He was tired of everyone's funny business!" is 12 words, but the counter shown runs "9, 6, 3, 0". (3) Beat 4 sets up "ten words" as the reserve, but the counter never shows 10, so the sharpest irony is missed: he spends exactly the ten he promised. In a story about counting words, these mismatches will be caught in the comments.
### giveaway

- finding: Beat 3 plants "From your belly, not your lips" and Mia's whistle-back. That's planting, not announcing: nothing says "this will save her". Repeating Gus's line in beat 9 happens at the climax itself, as a remembered voice. The climax is predictable (see originality), but no line hands the viewer the key before the crisis.

### originality
- applies: yes
- result: pass
- beats: 1, 3, 9, 10
- finding: The closest well-known story is "A Thousand Words" (2012: a man with a finite word supply learns what matters). The structural differences: (1) here the ration resets daily and is a whole society's rule, not one man's curse; (2) the crisis is a lost child, not a career or relationship lesson; (3) the solution is a non-verbal family call learned from a grandparent, not a spoken reconciliation. There's also a nod to "Whistle for Willie" (a boy finally learns to whistle), but only as a motif. More than the nouns differ.

## Overall
- keep-watching: yes. Beat 1's rule is a strong hook, and the counter ticking down to 0 against the promise (beat 5) creates real tension. The only dip is beat 2's list montage. A viewer would stay through to the reunion.
- verdict: revise
- top-fixes: 1. Fix the word arithmetic, the premise's credibility depends on it: make the woman in beat 7 silent (she taps her counter showing 2 and pulls her son close), and make Milo's counter read 10 as the joke starts, trimming the joke to exactly ten words ("Why did the clown quit? Tired of everyone's funny business!"), so he visibly spends the ten he promised. 2. Plant Mia's yellow coat in beats 4 and 5, and plant "at zero you go mute" in beat 1 or 2 (someone mouthing with no sound); also close off writing in beat 7 (Milo pats his empty pockets, or the guard shrugs at a gesture). 3.
