# Everyday Impossible #1: The Friday Pizza: screenplay (for the owner's approval)

**Status:** the story only. No production or rendering until the owner approves.

**The premise, in one sentence a stranger understands:** every Friday at seven, a pizza is delivered to the empty
house next door, and it's always paid for.

**The answer:** before she died, the old woman who lived there saw two kids eating cereal alone every Friday night
while their mum worked late. She paid for a year of Friday pizzas, delivered to her own empty house, because she knew
they'd find it.

**The twist:** her grandson is the delivery driver, and her husband writes the notes. When the year runs out, the kids
deliver a pizza to him.

**Runtime:** about 70–75 s (≈ 215 spoken words at ~3.2 words/s, plus three short held beats of under 1 s each).

**Setting:** Ashford, the same town as *One Too Many*. Two terraced houses side by side, Number 7 (ours) and Number 9
(the empty one), plus the pizza shop and the care home.

**Tone:** a mystery that turns warm. Nothing supernatural, no horror clichés. The dread at the start is just "who
could be paying for this?"

## Screenplay

**SCENE 1: The hook (0:00–0:05)**, the street at dusk (Blender)
_Frame 0: a moped headlight sweeps across a quiet terraced street at dusk, the camera already moving. A driver in a
red jacket walks a pizza box up the path of a dark house (Number 9: no lights, roses gone wild). He knocks. The camera
pushes past him to the black front window._
**Banner:** A PIZZA EVERY FRIDAY / FOR AN EMPTY HOUSE
> **MAYA (V.O.):** Every Friday at seven, a pizza gets delivered to the empty house next door. And it's always paid for.

**SCENE 2: Number 9 (0:05–0:11)**
_The camera pulls back along the dark windows. A soft, warm memory flash (a gentle dissolve): the same window in
daylight, Mrs Hale smiling and waving at something across the hedge. Back to the dark window._
> **MAYA (V.O.):** Number nine's been empty since old Mrs Hale died in the spring. Her husband's in a care home now.

**SCENE 3: Our kitchen, Friday night (0:11–0:18)**, Number 7 (a Blender interior)
_Mum's car headlights slide across the kitchen wall as she leaves. Maya and Theo at the table; two bowls of cereal.
Theo fishes out a single piece and inspects it._
> **MAYA (V.O.):** Mum works late on Fridays. So it's just me, my little brother Theo… and cereal.

**SCENE 4: The step (0:18–0:24)**, seen from our window
_Through our kitchen window: the driver waits on Number 9's step, checks his watch, then sets the box down and turns
it to face our window, deliberately. He rides off._
> **MAYA (V.O.):** The driver always knocks, waits one minute… then leaves the box on the step. Facing our window.

**SCENE 5: The heist (0:24–0:30)**
_Maya and Theo creep across the dark lawn, low behind the hedge. Maya lifts the lid: steam, the box light on their
faces._
> **MAYA (V.O.):** So one Friday, we took it.
> **MAYA (V.O.):** Half pepperoni. Half plain cheese. Theo only eats plain cheese.
> **THEO:** _(a whisper, impressed)_ It's like it knew.

**SCENE 6: The note (0:30–0:38)**
_Maya writes on a napkin and tapes it inside the empty box: "THANKS FOR THE PIZZA! – the kids at No. 7." She leaves it
on the step. A quick time-lapse: the box, night, the moped's light; the box is gone._
> **MAYA (V.O.):** We left a note in the empty box.
_Next Friday: a new box. Inside the lid, taped, a reply in shaky old handwriting._
> **MR HALE (V.O., reading):** You're very welcome. Same time next week. — H.
> **THEO:** H? Like… Hale?

**SCENE 7: The call (0:38–0:47)**, Maya's bedroom, her phone
_Maya on her bed, phone to her ear, the pizza shop's menu on screen: ROMANO'S._
> **MAYA (V.O.):** But Mrs Hale was gone. So I rang the pizza shop.
> **SHOP GIRL (phone):** Number nine? It's paid a year ahead. Paid in March… by a Mrs Hale.
_The camera pushes into Maya's face._
> **MAYA (V.O.):** March. The month before she died. **So why would she pay for a year of pizza for a house she'd never
> see again?**

**SCENE 8: The driver (0:47–0:59)**, the step, the next Friday
_Maya waits on Number 9's step this time. The moped pulls up; the driver takes off his helmet: young, kind._
> **SAM:** She was my nan. Every Friday she watched your mum's car leave… and saw you two eating cereal for dinner.
_The memory again, now complete: Mrs Hale at her window, looking across at the kids' lit kitchen, two small heads
over cereal bowls._
> **SAM:** She couldn't cook any more. So she ordered to her own house. She knew you'd find it.
> **MAYA (V.O.):** The pizza was never for the empty house. It was for us.
> **SAM:** Grandad writes the notes. I take him yours.

**SCENE 9: The care home (0:59–1:12)**, a warm room, Friday at seven
> **MAYA (V.O.):** Last Friday, the year ran out.
_A corridor in the care home. Maya, Theo and Mum (in her uniform) carry a Romano's box to a door. Mr Hale, in his
armchair, looks up as they come in. Theo lifts the lid._
> **MR HALE:** _(a smile spreading)_ Half plain cheese?
> **THEO:** That half's mine.
_The camera drifts back from the four of them sharing the pizza. The window behind them glows in the dusk, the same
light as frame 0._
> **MAYA (V.O.):** Now, every Friday at seven, a pizza gets delivered to Mr Hale. And it's always paid for.
_Cut to black on "for". The last line answers the first._

## Clarity block (each fact and the words that tell it)
- **The premise:** "Every Friday at seven, a pizza gets delivered to the empty house next door. And it's always paid for."
- **Why it's empty:** "Number nine's been empty since old Mrs Hale died in the spring. Her husband's in a care home now."
- **The kids are alone on Fridays:** "Mum works late on Fridays. So it's just me, my little brother Theo… and cereal."
- **The box was meant for them:** "leaves the box on the step. Facing our window." and "Theo only eats plain cheese."
- **Someone answers:** "You're very welcome. Same time next week. — H."
- **Who paid:** "It's paid a year ahead. Paid in March… by a Mrs Hale." / "The month before she died."
- **The question, out loud:** "So why would she pay for a year of pizza for a house she'd never see again?"
- **The answer:** "She watched your mum's car leave… and saw you two eating cereal for dinner… She knew you'd find it."
- **The meaning:** "The pizza was never for the empty house. It was for us."
- **The loop:** "Now, every Friday at seven, a pizza gets delivered to Mr Hale. And it's always paid for."

## Why each requirement is met
- **An extremely strong hook:** an everyday thing (a pizza delivery) plus one impossible detail (an empty house, always
  paid for), readable as the banner in under 2 s, with motion on frame 0.
- **Speculation:** "who is paying?" stays open until 0:47 (about 65 %), with several plausible wrong answers (a ghost,
  the driver, Mr Hale).
- **A fair explanation:** each step follows from a clue already shown.
- **The emotional twist:** the "empty house" mystery was really about two lonely kids, and the kindness comes back
  round.
- **The final line** mirrors the first line word for word, with the roles reversed.
