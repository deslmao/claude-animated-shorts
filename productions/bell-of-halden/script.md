# The Bell of Halden: premises, evaluation and script (for approval)

Format: "In this town...", narrated in the third person with in-scene dialogue. Target about 100 s.
Production constraint (owner): the existing pipeline, cameras, library and sound as they are. New set and prop
assets only where the story needs them, as for One Too Many's theme. No engine work.

## Five premises
| # | Opening line | The why | Turn and ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"In this town, a church bell rings every night at three. The town doesn't have a bell."** | The bell is in the church tower of Halden, the village drowned for the reservoir. It rings when the dam's 3 a.m. release moves the water. | One night it rings an hour early: the water is moving when nothing should be. The dam is failing. The new girl, the only one who never got used to it, wakes the street. At dawn the drained valley shows the bell-ringer had tied his bell to the river on purpose. |
| 2 | "In this town, the post office still delivers letters to a man who died in 1962. And every week, he writes back." | The whole town takes turns writing as Frank to his widow, Edie, who has dementia. | When Edie dies, a letter of hers is found: "I knew from the second letter. Frank couldn't spell." The clue is planted in Frank's misspelt 1962 postcard. |
| 3 | "In this town, at 9:03 every night, everyone stops and looks at the hill." | The old fire watcher blinks an all-clear lamp from her tower. | She died years ago and the lamp is on a timer. The night the hill burns, it still says all clear. |
| 4 | "In this town, nothing can be built taller than the oak on the hill." | Fishermen steer through the rocks by lining the oak up with the church spire. | A developer's tower goes up; in the first fog, a boat lines up the wrong light. |
| 5 | "In this town, every compass points to the bakery." | An iron meteorite under the ovens. | Comic: the bakery family has been hiding it for 150 years. |

| 5 The compass | 4 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 19 |

- **2** has the best emotional twist, but letters from a dead husband are a familiar idea. The stakes stay quiet, and it's mostly interiors and paper.
- **3** works, but it uses the same mechanism as One Too Many: a check that says "all fine" when it isn't. Our viewers would see the pattern.
- **4** and **5** have a good why, but weak endings: once the reason is known, the rest is predictable.

## Recommendation: 1, The Bell of Halden
- **Why the hook works:** the first line is an impossible fact (a bell in a town with no bell), so the why is immediate.
- **Two turns that each change the meaning:**
  1. The explanation (the drowned village, the nightly release) is satisfying on its own at the halfway point.
  2. The explanation then becomes the warning: an early ring means the water is moving.
- **Every clue pays off:**
  - "Only when the water moves" leads to the early ring.
  - "He swore the dam would give way" leads to the rope.
  - "The only one awake" and "still woke every night" pay off in "one person who hadn't got used to it".
  - The empty belfry in the first shot is filled in the last one.
- **Visual set pieces:**
  - a church tower standing in a reservoir;
  - a sleeping valley town at night;
  - a storm and a power cut;
  - a torchlit climb up the hill;
  - a drained valley at dawn with the drowned village in the mud.

## Critique of the first draft, and the one revision
1. **Too much exposition.** The draft narrated Halden's whole history and the resettlement. The revision keeps one line of history and lets the picture show the rest (the tower in the water).
2. **The payoff missed.** In the draft the bell rang early at 9 p.m., with everyone awake, so the hook's "the only one awake" paid nothing off. It now rings at 1:52, when the town is asleep and only Nia notices.
3. **A logic gap: why the dam's own alarms didn't go off.** The storm cuts the power: the streetlights go out on screen. That costs no words.
4. **Unclear stakes.** Nia's house is set up on "the street by the river" in beat 2 and is the street that floods in beat 10.
5. **A double ending.** The draft ended on two closing lines. The revision ends on one line, then the image of Halden's bell in the town's empty belfry, closing the loop on the first shot.
6. **Feasibility (current system).**
   - The bell hangs in a tower that stands above the water, so no underwater shots are needed. The rope runs down inside the tower into the water, which is why nobody has seen where it goes.
   - The flood is told by sound, the view from the hill, and the drowned street at dawn: no rushing-water effect.

## Final script
**N** = narrator. Times are estimates.

| # | Time | Picture | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00 | Night. A valley town asleep under the hills, every window dark. A deep bell tolls. Cut to the town church's belfry: empty, moonlight through it. | **N:** In this town, a church bell rings every night at three. ⋯ The town doesn't have a bell. |
| 2 | 0:07 | The lowest street, by the river: one lit window. Nia (12) at it, in pyjamas, listening. | **N:** Nia had just moved in, to the street by the river. On her first night, she was the only one awake to hear it. |
| 3 | 0:15 | Morning. Back gardens. Mrs Wynn (80s) pegs out washing over the fence. | **NIA:** Did you hear that bell last night? **MRS WYNN:** (not looking up) That's just Halden, love. **NIA:** What's Halden? **MRS WYNN:** (nods up the valley at the dam) Under that. |
| 4 | 0:24 | The reservoir behind the dam. In the middle of the water, the top of a church tower, a bell in its open belfry. | **N:** Halden was the village in this valley, before they flooded it to make the reservoir. They took everything out of the church except the bell. |
| 5 | 0:33 | Back at the fence; Mrs Wynn stops pegging. | **MRS WYNN:** My dad rang that bell for forty years. Swore that dam would give way one day. **NIA:** So who rings it now? **MRS WYNN:** Nobody's been out there in sixty years. It only rings when the water moves. |
| 6 | 0:43 | Nia on her bike at the dam. Insert: the sign TURBINE RELEASE 03:00 DAILY. Night: the water slides toward the dam, and out in the tower the bell swings. | **N:** Every night at three, the dam lets water through its turbines, and the current swings Halden's bell. |
| 7 | 0:50 | Three quick nights: Nia's eyes open; her phone reads 03:00; she rolls over and smiles. | **N:** The whole town slept through it. Nia still woke up every night at three. |
| 8 | 0:55 | A storm. The streetlights go out, street by street. Nia wakes; the bell is ringing. Insert: her phone, 01:52. | **N:** Then, one stormy night, it rang an hour early. **NIA:** (whisper) Nothing's meant to move till three. **NIA:** (shaking Dad) Dad. The bell's early. **DAD:** (into the pillow) It's the storm, Nia. Go back to sleep. |
| 9 | 1:06 | Rain. Nia hammers on Mrs Wynn's door. It opens; Mrs Wynn listens. The bell is ringing faster. | **MRS WYNN:** The water's moving. ⋯ Wake the street. |
| 10 | 1:12 | Doors, torches, coats over pyjamas; the street climbs the hill road in the rain. A neighbour grumbles. Then a roar from up the valley. From the hill, torches turn to look. | **NEIGHBOUR:** It's two in the morning! **MRS WYNN:** Keep walking. **N:** The bell rang faster, and faster. ⋯ At ten past two, the dam gave way. By morning, the street by the river was under water. Nobody was in it. |
| 11 | 1:28 | Dawn. The reservoir has drained. Halden stands in the mud: roofs, walls, the church. Nia and Mrs Wynn at the foot of the tower. A rope runs from the bell, down inside the tower, to an iron ring set in the old riverbed. | **N:** With the water gone, they could finally see why. Halden's bell-ringer had tied his bell to the river. **MRS WYNN:** (smiling, wet-eyed) Forty years he rang it. He wasn't going to stop for a reservoir. |
| 12 | 1:40 | Nia looks up at the bell. | **N:** For sixty years, it had tested its alarm every night at three. It only needed one person who hadn't got used to it. |
| 13 | 1:46 | The town church. In the belfry that was empty in the first shot, Halden's bell now hangs. It tolls once. Cut to black. | *(silent)* |

**Length:** about 265 words (178 narrated, 87 spoken in scene), so about 100–108 s with the silent beats.

**Voices:**
- **Narrator:** georgeHushed, the channel's mystery narrator.
- **Nia:** a young British girl's voice.
- **Mrs Wynn:** an older woman, warm and dry; she could be Welsh, which suits a drowned valley.
- **Dad:** brian.
- **Neighbour:** any existing male preset.

All five need auditioning before generation.
