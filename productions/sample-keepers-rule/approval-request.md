# The Keeper's Rule: story approval

**Format:** immersive + mystery. The viewer keeps the lighthouse and must choose; each side gets evidence the viewer can check (a log entry with a visible cost, a radar fix, a voice that shouldn't exist), and the margin note about a green light becomes evidence for both sides once a real boat's green starboard light appears.

**Estimated length:** 72 s · **Words:** 210 · **Narration:** 1128 characters (about 1128 ElevenLabs credits) · **Voices:** narrator=georgeHushed, nurse=matilda, gran=jessica, radio=chris, coastguard=alice

## Premise
- **question:** Is the boat calling for light real, or is it the thing your gran's rule warns about?
- **protagonist:** you (the viewer), keeping your gran's lighthouse for one night while she is in hospital
- **want:** to keep your promise to Gran, and to keep people safe
- **fear:** getting it wrong either way: people drown, or you let something in
- **protects:** the crew on the boat, and yourself
- **decision:** switch the lamp back on, or keep it dark as the rule says
- **conflict:** A, a real fishing boat is drifting onto the rocks and needs the light; B, the bell rang by itself, and the logbook shows that something calls for light on these nights, and that lighting the lamp once cost a life

## Independent evaluation
**Verdict:** revise → not ready · **Would a viewer keep watching?** yes. The hook (a sick gran, a strange promise) and the bell swinging beside a still flag hold attention, and every beat adds a new fact up to a real choice. The likeliest drop-off is the cluttered beat 1 (three voices before the rule lands) and the dense logbook reading in beats 5 and 6. Viewers who reach beat 9 and miss that Gran is Ellen will finish confused rather than chilled.
