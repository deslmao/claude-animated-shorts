## Strengths and weaknesses
### A
- strengths: Clear geography from the opening wide (1). Faces carry the emotion muted (Mira at 5 and 9; Asha's steady gaze at 8). The cause of the fourth picture is visible ("picks up her brush" 10). "She's on the bench" (14) is usefully ambiguous: both artists are sitting there.
- weaknesses: The narrator knows what Mira did and holds it back until 9, which is the convention, but it makes the reveal something the story tells us rather than something we find. "So did she." (9) judges from outside. The viewer watches the guilt instead of feeling it. "Three figures laughing" (4) mostly describes the shot.
### B
- strengths: The narrator doesn't know either: she has forgotten, and that forgetting is the cruelty ("You don't remember, do you?" 8). So the withholding is honest, and it means something. "With my paint." (6) and "my signature" (5, 11) become ironic after "Because it's yours." (8). One frame with both straps (9). "So did I." is a confession (9). The only outside shot comes when she sits beside Asha (14).
- weaknesses: No establishing geography (1). The protagonist has no face or name until 14. The muted emotional reads are thin (5, 10). The cause of the fourth picture is lost (10). The viewer holds the camera all film, so they may guess the camera link at 5. "I'm on the bench" (14) loses A's double meaning and could read as Mira claiming to be the artist of Asha's panels.

## Where the perspective changes the experience
- **Beat 6 to beat 8 ("With my paint." / "Because it's yours.")**: in B, the viewer takes on Mira's grievance in her own words, so Asha's answer turns back on the viewer's own sense of being wronged. A's "With the mural's own paint." is neutral, so the line in 8 explains Asha without wrong-footing the viewer. **B works better.**
- **Beat 8 (Asha "looks up into the lens, steady")**: in B, "Nobody sees me" and "You don't remember, do you?" are said to the viewer, who has also passed her without seeing her (2). In A the viewer overhears the same lines. **B works better.** This is the main gain of the experiment.
- **Beat 9 (the realisation)**: A cuts from the photo to the painted strap to Mira's draining face, so the viewer is told what to feel. B keeps the real strap in the foreground with the painting behind it and has no face, so the viewer makes the match and supplies the feeling. **B works better for an attentive viewer; A is safer for someone half-watching or muted.**
- **Beat 10 (walking away)**: A shows Asha watching her go and then picking up her brush, which is both an emotional beat and the cause of 11. In B the eyes-front walk expresses shame well with sound ("my breathing"), but it drops Asha and the cause. **A works better.** The perspective causes the loss, but a single look back would fix it.
- **Beat 5 (Okafor's threat)**: in A, Mira's falling face sells what the threat costs her. In B the threat is said to the lens, which is immersive but shows nothing of the stake. **A works slightly better.**
- **Beat 14 (the ending)**: B's first and only outside shot, after a film where "nobody sees" anyone, lets the viewer finally see Mira, sitting where she should. That's a stronger formal close than A's pull-out. But "I'm on the bench" replaces A's ambiguous "She's on the bench" (Asha or Mira, both artists) with a claim by Mira. **Mixed:** B's picture is better and A's line is better.

## Fairness check
The comparison is mostly fair: same beats, same plot fixes, near-identical lines and the same sound plan. There are a few differences the perspective doesn't explain:
- **B has extra on-screen information:** MONDAY · 7:30 AM, TUESDAY · 7:30 AM and WEDNESDAY cards (1, 4, 5), where A relies on narration. They help B's timeline and the 7:30 deduction (6) without being a property of first person. A could have them too.
- **B has an extra clue detail at beat 6:** the fresh thumbprint "next to my own thumb", a small size comparison that A lacks.
- **The wording at beat 4 differs:** A "Three figures laughing" (flagged as an 80% restatement of the shot) against B "Three of them laughing" (not flagged). B's wording is slightly better and isn't required by the perspective.
- **A has an extra giveaway flag at beat 1.** It's a heuristic word-overlap artefact of the "Mira" phrasing, not a real difference.
- **A shot B could have kept: Asha picking up the brush at 10.** POV caused the loss, but the storyboard chose to drop it ("I don't turn my head") rather than keep it with a glance back.

None of these decides the result. Removing the day cards from B (or adding them to A) narrows the clarity gap slightly but changes no other row.

## Verdict
- does first person genuinely improve the experience: mixed. First person improves the part of this story that matters most: the realisation at 8 and 9 implicates the viewer, the narrator's forgetting becomes the point, and the single outside shot at 14 is earned. The cost is in the basics a Shorts viewer relies on: no geography at 1, no protagonist face until 14, thin muted reads at 5 and 10, and a lost causal shot at 10. It also risks the camera link being guessed even earlier than in A.
- which to make, if only one: B, because its gains land on the reveal, which is why this story exists, and its losses are craft gaps that small storyboard changes can close.
- what first person would need to win clearly: (1) At 10, one involuntary look back, the only time "I" turns my head, catching Asha picking up her brush. That restores the cause of 11 and turns the avoidance into a visible flinch. (2) Show Mira's face once without leaving POV: her reflection in the dark camera screen as her thumb stops at 9 (or in the hall door's glass at 7), and add a slow head pan across mural, bench and hall door at 1 for the geography.
