# Viral breakdown: "Mr. Gray is visiting today" (school headcount horror)

**What it is:** a reference video the owner supplied (17 Nov 2026), analysed from the file itself.
- 720×1280, 30 fps, **2:10**.
- Frames every 2 s, a speech-to-text transcript with speakers and tagged sound events, and a loudness and spectrum
  measurement every 2 s.
- **Technique only. Never copy the story, characters or lines.**

## The one-line premise
The school intercom says a "Mr. Gray is visiting", which is a code: someone in the class isn't real. The class counts
heads in the dark. By the end, the boy whose eyes we see through (Eli) is the one who doesn't belong.

## Narration and voices
- **No narrator at all.** It's a fully voiced scene with **5 distinct voices** (speaker diarisation):
  - **the intercom:** a calm, official adult voice, filtered, with a chime before it and feedback screeches;
  - **the teacher:** authoritative; explains the code and does the count;
  - **Eli (the POV):** a scared child; breathing and panting;
  - **Mum, on the phone:** frantic, in a phone-quality voice;
  - **a classmate:** teasing ("Eli, *scoff*, you came in late").
- **Pace:** **183 words in 2:10, about 84 words a minute** (our narrated films: 150–160). The gaps are long: 6.9 s of
  footsteps, 7.8 s of phone vibrating, 2–3.5 s pauses in the count and after "It's him."
- **Why the silences don't lose viewers** (unlike *The Extra Door*'s 10 s):
  - every silence is **diegetic tension sound plus an action on screen**: footsteps as someone walks in, the phone
    buzzing while texts appear, breathing in the dark;
  - **nothing is ever just a held picture.**

## Picture
- **Camera:**
  - **first-person POV** for Eli: his hands on the desk, the worksheet, the phone held in front of him. The viewer *is*
    Eli without anyone saying "you";
  - over-the-shoulder shots and close-ups of faces;
  - a slow push onto the intercom speaker;
  - wide, still shots of the dark class.
- **Lighting arc (the most important visual device):**
  1. bright, flat, pastel daylight (safe);
  2. "Lights off": everything drops to **navy blue**. Characters become silhouettes **with white glowing eyes**
     (the image from the first frame returns);
  3. the phone screen lights Eli's hands;
  4. at the twist, light through the window blinds falls in stripes across Eli.
- **On-screen text:**
  - **captions colour-coded by speaker:** yellow/orange for the adults and the intercom, green/teal for Mum and the
    kids, white in brackets for his thoughts;
  - a top-left time and place card ("NINE MINUTES EARLIER · Friday · 2:51 PM · Room 108");
  - a phone UI overlay (texts, incoming call, an on-call timer counting up 00:01… 00:17, CALL ENDED).
- **Cutting:** a new shot every 2–6 s; the intercom and the phone get longer holds, but something always moves (the
  ring pulsing, texts arriving, the timer ticking).
- **Style:** simple 3D flat-shaded characters (the same family as ours: big heads, dot eyes, no fine detail). Quality
  isn't the reason it works.

## Sound
- **Effects (tagged by the transcription):**
  - footsteps;
  - the intercom chime;
  - the intercom feedback screech (twice);
  - panting and breathing;
  - the phone vibrating;
  - the phone ringing;
  - thunder (on "It's him");
  - classroom room tone.
- **Music:**
  - **almost none in the daylight section.** That section is the quietest (down to -27 to -33 dB in the gaps): room
    tone, voices, footsteps;
  - **a low drone from the cold open and the dark scenes on** (below 150 Hz: 50–75% of the energy at 0–4 s and
    0:46–0:48, up to 93% under the phone texts);
  - **a long build over the last 35 s.** From the count at 1:36 to the end, the low end rises to 70–100% of the
    energy, and the level climbs about 4 dB (-15.5 dB to -11.7 dB) into the phone ring.
- **The mix:** the voices are always on top; the drone sits under them. There's real dynamic range: quiet daylight,
  loud ending.

## Why it went viral (inferred)
1. **The first frame and first line are a rule and a secret** ("Do not let them know you counted") over glowing eyes
   in the dark. You need the context, so you stay.
2. **A countdown and a code word:** "nine minutes", "Mr. Gray". Concrete, school-real (everyone knows lockdown drills),
   and instantly understood.
3. **Rules horror,** stated simply and repeated.
4. **A count the viewer joins** (21… 22… 23… 24), the same device as our *One Too Many*.
5. **Two escalating twists, the second turning on the viewer's own POV character.** The clue at 0:17 rewards a
   rewatch, and the "wait, who came in late?" ambiguity drives comments.
6. **Phone realism:** texts and a call from Mum. Everyone knows that dread.
7. **Real performances from five voices** make it feel like a film, not a reading.
8. **The light carries the fear:** day to dark to eyes, and it's readable at feed size.
9. **The ending:** a silent choice card after the climax, with no narrated call to action and no story time wasted.
