# Reference clip 3: comedic framing (technique only)

**Source:** the screen recording sent on 6 Nov 2026 (12.4 s, 1206 × 2622, 60 fps). It is a Short about a cashier and
a winning lottery ticket. No frames are committed, and none of its characters, scenes or script are reused.
**Method:** contact sheet at 4 frames / s, and the mean frame-to-frame change at 30 fps.

## What it does
- **About 12 compositions in 12.4 s, with no hard cuts.** Every change is a fast move or a quick dissolve.
- **The energy follows the joke.** Motion is high in the set-up (18-36 in the first 2 s), then the camera goes nearly
  still for the deadpan punchlines (1.2-4).
- **A swoop to a macro insert on the noun.** On "ticket", the camera rushes from a medium shot past the character's
  shoulder into an extreme close-up of the ticket in his hand, then pulls back.
- **In-world information.** The amount ("a million dollars") appears on the cash register's display, not as a
  floating panel.
- **A punch-in on the reaction.** On "a million", an extreme close-up of the shocked face, eyes wide.
- **A joke with no words.** A held close-up where only the eyes dart sideways (a glance) says "he's going to lie"
  before he speaks.
- **A deadpan hold.** A static close-up on "Not a winner": stillness is the punchline.
- **An over-the-shoulder reaction.** The other character, framed past a soft foreground shoulder, shrugs ("story of
  my life") and walks out. The depth comes from the foreground blur.
- **A comic camera position for the payoff.** The camera sits low beside the trash can as the cashier's arm dives in,
  "elbow deep".
- **An iris close.** The last line plays in a circular vignette around the smug face (a cartoon "iris in").

## Against Thinkora's current system
- **Already there:**
  - word-timed beats, whips, glides, flights;
  - `closeUp` with `punch`;
  - `insert` on props / `actor.held` / `actor.face` with size `extreme`;
  - `overShoulder`, `pov`, `lowHero`, `dutch`;
  - controlled blur and camera clearance.
- **Missing:**
  - a fast eased "swoop" into a macro insert, with an arrival settle;
  - a macro size for small and held objects that stays readable and clear of scenery;
  - eye-glance beats;
  - an iris punchline;
  - in-world text displays (signs, screens, scoreboards);
  - a rhythm check (long static stretches during narration, or every shot equally energetic).
