# Locked Out: story approval

**Format:** mystery. A first-person mystery told through a locksmith's hands and trade knowledge: the viewer checks each piece of evidence with me (an ID, a dog, a drawing, a lock that's too new), is addressed by the man at the door, and reaches the decision at the same moment I do.

**Estimated length:** 71 s · **Words:** 207 · **Narration:** 1057 characters (about 1057 ElevenLabs credits) · **Voices:** narrator=liamQuiet, marsh=brian, me=liamQuiet, operator=alice, woman=jessica

## Premise
- **question:** At 2:14 a.m. a man is paying me double to open his own front door, quietly. Is it really his door, and why has his key stopped working?
- **protagonist:** me, a night-call locksmith on a final written warning
- **want:** to do the job right, and keep the job
- **fear:** being wrong either way: opening a home to the wrong man, or leaving an honest man out in the cold again
- **protects:** whoever is behind the door
- **decision:** to go back to the door and jam the lock for good, instead of opening it or driving away
- **loses:** my job, if I wrongly refuse him (shown: the final warning on my van's visor); someone's safety, if I wrongly open
- **obstacle:** every check he passes is genuine. The truth isn't a lie he tells; it's something he leaves out
- **conflict:** A, he's an honest man locked out of his own home after a row; B, he's not meant to be let in, and I'm the way in

## Independent evaluation
**Verdict:** ready → ready · **Would a viewer keep watching?** yes. The hook (break into his own house) grips at once. The spike comes at beat 6, a bright new lock in flaking paint with filings on the mat ("uh-oh"). Beats 8 and 9, "It's one of ours" and "Everything he's told me is true", are the moment people rewatch. The brick and the hand over the horn (11), then "Found them! One minute." with 999 in his pocket (12), carry the second half on a real clock. The likeliest swipe point is beat 3 (about 0:08 to 0:14): three lines of rule, backstory and complaint over a slow ID check. Keep it tight and visual, with the licence beside the brass 9 and the glance at the letter. A second, smaller dip is possible at beat 4 (ringing the bell) if it's held. The ending (the woman's look, the THE LOCK MAN drawing over the warning letter) is warm and shareable. Likely top comment: the final line quoted, "'I checked who he was. I never asked who the lock was for.' Chills." Top critical comment: "He had a brick the whole time. Why'd he waste a minute on the broken pick?" This is a story a real viewer would finish and pass on, not just one that technically passes.
