# Research: rail expansion gaps (sample, NOT verified)

## Summary
Jointed rail leaves gaps for thermal expansion. Modern continuous welded rail (CWR) is laid without gaps, under tension
at a chosen neutral temperature and restrained by sleepers, fastenings and ballast. These are claims to verify, not facts yet.

## Claims to verify (no sources yet)
- The linear expansion coefficient of rail steel is about 11–12 × 10⁻⁶ per °C, so a 20 m rail over a 40 °C swing changes length by about 9–10 mm.
- Unrestrained or poorly restrained track can buckle sideways in heat (a "sun kink").
- CWR is installed at a stress-free (neutral) temperature, using rail heaters or hydraulic tensors, then fastened.
