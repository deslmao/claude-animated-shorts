# Visual Storytelling 2.0: what the reference clip actually does

**Source:** the screen recording you sent on 4 Nov 2026. It is a 12.5 s excerpt of the YouTube Short "Why Perfect
Railroads Don't Work" (@RicksClips3000), recorded at 832 × 1498 and 60 fps.

**How I studied it:**
- contact sheets at 2 and 4 frames / s;
- a frame-to-frame change measurement at 30 fps, to tell cuts from camera moves.

**Limits:**
- The recording has **no audio track**, so the sound design isn't assessed here.
- This file describes **technique only**. No frames of the clip are committed to the repository, and none of its
  scenes, characters or designs will be copied.

## What it means for Thinkora (measured, not guessed)
1. **The camera is almost never still.** Frame-to-frame change stays above the hold level for about 11 of the 12.5 s.
   There is **only one hard cut** (2.5 s) and two dissolves.
   - New information comes from **moving through the world**: a pull-back reveal, a fly-over, a tilt into the abyss,
     a dive through a wall, a forward flight.
   - More cuts are not where the new information comes from.
   - This corrects my first slice, which leaned on hard-cut inserts.
2. **A new picture about every 0.5-1.5 s, matched to the words.**
   - "racing to build": the two half-bridges;
   - "so deep": the void below a plank;
   - "whoever finishes first": the race across the desert;
   - "the whole country's business": the station and the tycoon.
3. **The environment is the explanation.**
   - Nothing is explained with a panel.
   - The idea of a race is shown as unfinished structures in a vast place.
   - Money is shown as a person on a heap of gold, under a banner that states the stakes.
4. **Transitions are journeys.** The camera travels from one place to the next (wall → desert → town → station), so
   each new idea arrives as a new place.
5. **Scale contrast.** Small figures in huge spaces (the bridges, the plank over the void), then big faces for the
   punchlines (the engineer, the wink).
6. **Fast moves are motion-blurred,** which makes speed read as speed rather than as judder.
7. **The characters are as simple as ours.** Stick limbs, round heads, flat colour. The gap is the camera, the
   world's depth and scale, and the world-as-explanation. The character art is not the gap.
8. **Captions:** 1-2 words, centred, white with a black outline, in about the same place as ours.

## Changes to the 2.0 slice (no change to what you approved, only to emphasis)
- **P1 / P2: camera beats.**
  - Weight: continuous **flights** (multi-point glides through the set, pull-back reveals, tilt-downs, forward flights)
    over cut-ins.
  - Inserts stay, but as one tool among several.
  - Glides already chain beat to beat. I'll add:
    - a deterministic **camera motion blur** for fast moves (measured for render cost, as P4 requires);
    - a **fly-through transition** that carries the camera into one set and out into the next (the wall → desert move).
- **P3: kits.**
  - Prefer **in-world** explanations: the loot heap splitting on the deck, a harbour of owners' ships for "owners on
    land", the crew's raised hands seen from the captain's side.
  - Keep panels for short comparisons only.
- **Pirate pilot:** the scenes will be planned as journeys around the ship and out to sea / the harbour, not as a
  string of set-ups.
