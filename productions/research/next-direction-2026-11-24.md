# Thinkora: the next content direction (24 Nov 2026)

Evidence first, opinions second. These sources are numbered and cited below:
- Thinkora's own YouTube Studio data (`winning-patterns.md`);
- the frame-by-frame and script study of Rick's clips (`STUDY.md` §1–7);
- external research on successful animated Shorts channels.

## 1. What actually worked on Thinkora

| Short | Views | Stayed to watch | Avg viewed | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **One Too Many** | ~21–23K | **85.5 %** | **79.6 %** | a narrated mystery in a school: one impossible **number** (31 pupils, register says 30), a **logical answer** (a boy's joke in a 1979 fire made the count "right"), and an ending that **loops** (the count becomes 32) |
| Mum's Making Breakfast | 1,354 (13.5K on Instagram) | 70.9 % | 67 % | an everyday situation, then an impossible detail (her text says she's at work), with the answer held back |
| Whale City | — | 75.9 % | 54.5 % | a world-rule story, with long silences |
| Explainers (doorway, tickle, marathon, pirates) | 1–2K | 52–68 % | 50–73 % | the hook is answered at once, so there's no reason to stay |
| The Last Train | 562 | 51 % | 50 % | a scream hook with no context |
| Stop Time Pt 1, Nova, the memories video | 231–928 | 26–45 % | — | invented sci-fi worlds that need explaining, "Part 1" labels, vote cards |

**The pattern, stated plainly:**
- **The winners open on an everyday place with one impossible, concrete detail** (a count, a text, a time).
- **They hold the question,** answer it with a *logical* explanation, and twist the ending.
- **The losers are invented worlds** (too much set-up), explainers (the answer comes too early), scream hooks (no
  picture of the situation) and multi-part cliffhangers (new viewers arrive at Part 2 lost, and the vote card makes
  people leave: *Extra Door* fell from 29.7 % to 13.4 % on its call to action).

## 2. What the successful animated Shorts channels do

| Channel | Scale | Format | Why viewers return |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zack D. Films** | ~29M subscribers, ~81B views, 100 % Shorts, since 2021 [1][2] | 3D animated "dark curiosities": what happens if…, why you should never… | one unmistakable look and voice, and the **same promise every time** (you'll learn a strange true thing in 60 s); endless topics |
| **Rick's clips** | its 15 newest Shorts ~167M views, 5 over 10M (Nov 2026, a promo source, treat with care) [3]; our own study of 5 Shorts | 2D cast in a detailed 3D world; a **15-word paradox opener**, a cause-and-effect chain, the question at ~65 %, **a real-world mechanism as the answer**, no outro | the "*how* is that possible?" itch, scratched every time; the format never changes, the topic always does |
| **SCP Explained** | ~2.4M subscribers, ~0.9B views [4] | animated adaptations of one shared fictional universe, numbered seasons | **a shared world**: every episode adds to a universe fans already know |
| **TheOdd1sOut / Domics** | 20M+ / millions [5] | personal stories about everyday life | a **recurring narrator personality**; relatability |
| Haegens (Dutch animation) | a Google case study [6] | short "Top 3" Shorts feeding an animated series | Shorts brought **returning viewers**, who then found the series |

**What these channels have in common:**
- None of them depends on a trend.
- Each makes **one repeatable promise** in one recognisable package (look, voice, opening line), with a topic supply
  that never runs out.
- Loyalty comes from **knowing what you'll get**: a recurring format, a recurring voice, sometimes a shared world.
- Art style is secondary: stickman channels get millions. Story and packaging decide it (our 20 Nov research).
- YouTube itself is now pushing **habit over virality**: Shorts seasons, episodes, and sequential playback [7][8].

## 3. The directions, scored (1–5, 5 is best)

| Direction | Viral | Retention | Returning | Endless ideas | Competition (5 = little) | Fits our pipeline | Long-term | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Everyday Impossible: logic mysteries with a human answer** (the One Too Many lane) | 4 | **5** | 4 | 4 | **4** | **5** | **5** | **31** |
| B. True "why it happened" story-explainers (the Rick's clips / Zack D lane) | **5** | 4 | 4 | **5** | 1 | 4 | 5 | 28 |
| C. "Imagine…" POV dilemmas with a choice ending (the breakfast lane) | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 5 | 3 | 25 |
| D. Thought experiments ("What if everyone could hear thoughts?") | 3 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 21 |
| E. Horror with unexpected humour (6 7) | 3 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 20 |
| F. Comedy / relatable sketches | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 20 |
| G. Psychology / mindset explainers | 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 1 | 4 | 3 | 18 |
| H. Multi-part vote series as the main format | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 20 |
| ✗ GTA / trend comedy | 4 now, 1 later | 3 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 4 | 1 | 16 |

**Brutal notes on the weak ones:**
- **G. Psychology explainers:** our own data killed this. They answer their hook immediately (52–68 % stayed), and the
  space is full of faster, cheaper channels.
- **D. Thought experiments:** they sound clever but have no story engine. Nothing is at stake, there's no ending to
  wait for, and "what if" channels already saturate it.
- **E. Horror-comedy:** the tone is hard to hit every time. When the joke lands, the scare doesn't, and the reverse.
  Fine as an occasional flavour, not an identity.
- **H. Vote series:**
  - the evidence is against making it the core: Stop Time Pt 1 got 231 views, the vote card lost half the audience,
    and Part 2s inherit only the Part 1 audience;
  - keep the vote as a comment prompt inside complete stories, never as an unfinished ending.
- **F. Comedy sketches:** they depend on writing jokes daily, competition is enormous, and they don't use our pipeline's
  strength (atmosphere, camera, sound).
- **B. True story-explainers:**
  - the strongest *market*, but we'd be fighting Rick's clips and Zack D on their home ground;
  - we measured both as ahead of us in motion and acting;
  - every video also needs careful fact-checking, and one wrong fact can sink trust.

  Use its *structure* (the paradox opener, the chain, the held question), not its lane.

## 4. The strongest direction: **A. "Everyday Impossible"**

**The promise, the same every time:** *Something impossible is happening somewhere completely ordinary. There's always
a reason, and it's never the one you expect.*

**Why this one:**
1. **It's the format our best video already is.** One Too Many's 85.5 % stayed and 79.6 % average viewed is the
   strongest evidence we have, and it's exactly this. Breakfast (71 %) is the same pattern at home.
2. **It's original and uncrowded.** Horror channels do monsters and doors. Fact channels do real phenomena. Almost no
   one does *fictional everyday mysteries with a satisfying logical answer*. It's the "how is that possible?" itch of
   Rick's clips, in stories we invent.
3. **It builds return visits for reasons beyond the algorithm:**
   - **A game:** viewers try to solve it before the reveal. The pinned comment: "The clue was on screen at 0:12. Did
     you catch it?"
   - **A shared town:** every story happens in one place (Ashford, from One Too Many, is already ours). The school, the
     bus, the station, the corner shop, the same caretaker and bus driver in the background. Regulars spot the
     connections, as SCP fans do.
   - **One recognisable package:**
     - the same narrator (George, hushed);
     - the same opening shape: an ordinary place, then one impossible number;
     - the same closing shape: a twist that loops to the first line.
4. **The ideas never run out.** Anything countable or measurable can go wrong: a count, a weight, a time, a number of
   steps, a temperature, a receipt total. Each one generates a story.
5. **It suits everyone in our audience** (35–44 is 29 %, 13–17 is 23 %, 25–34 is 22 %, US-led) and stays
   advertiser-friendly: dread and wonder, not gore. Some answers are eerie, many are human and touching, a few are
   funny. That variety keeps it from becoming predictable.
6. **The pipeline already does it:** the Blender town, the narrator, the captions, the score. No rebuild.

**Where this direction can go wrong, and the guard for each:**
- **Making every answer a ghost** (One Too Many again and again). Rule: at most 1 in 3 answers is supernatural. The
  rest are human, clever or bittersweet.
- **Weak logic:** the answer has to be fair. The clue must be shown before the reveal.
- **Losing the pace:** the STUDY.md rules apply:
  - a 15-word opener;
  - a "You see / So / But / Then" chain;
  - no silence over about 1.5 s;
  - the question answered at ~65 %, then the twist, then the loop.

## 5. Five original concepts (Everyday Impossible)

1. **"The Friday Pizza."**
   - **Premise:** every Friday, a pizza is delivered to the empty house at the end of our street, and someone always
     pays.
   - **The answer:** an old man who sold the house pays for it, because his late wife always ordered pizza there on
     Fridays.
   - **The twist:** the new owners' kids have been eating it for months and leaving thank-you notes, which he collects
     from the doorstep.
2. **"3 A.M. Roses."**
   - **Premise:** my neighbour's sprinkler comes on at 3 a.m. every night, and he's been in hospital for a month.
   - **The answer:** his dog learned to turn the outside tap, because it's the only time it ever saw him: he watered
     the roses at 3 a.m. after night shifts.
   - **The twist:** the roses are blooming for the day he comes home.
3. **"The Full Shelf."**
   - **Premise:** the library loses one book every night, yet every morning the shelves are full again.
   - **The answer:** a kid with nowhere to go has been reading one book a night and returning it.
   - **The twist:** the librarian has known for two years. She's been leaving a new book on the returns trolley every
     night, in his reading order.
4. **"Minus One Degree."**
   - **Premise:** classroom 4B is always exactly one degree colder than the rest of the school, even in summer.
   - **The answer:** the old caretaker set the thermostat for a pupil who overheats from a medical condition.
   - **The twist:** that pupil is now the head teacher, and she's never let anyone change it.
   - This one has no ghost, and all of it is a logic trail.
5. **"The 41st Step."**
   - **Premise:** the station stairs have 40 steps, and every commuter who counts going down gets 41.
   - **The answer:** an extra step was built in 1962, after a fall, to slow people down, and it was painted to blend
     in.
   - **The twist:** the narrator counts going *up* and gets 39.
   - This is the one eerie answer, and the loop sets up a follow-up.

## 6. Three opening hooks (15 words or fewer, everyday place, impossible detail)
1. "Every Friday, a pizza gets delivered to the empty house on our street. Someone always pays."
2. "My neighbour's sprinkler turns on at 3 a.m. every night. He's been in hospital for a month."
3. "The library loses one book every night. By morning, the shelves are always full again."

## 7. A realistic 10-video test (one a day, all the same format)
- **The rule:** don't change the format during the test. Change **one variable per pair**, and judge on the data, not
  on one video's views.
- **The label:** every video is "Everyday Impossible #N". Same narrator, same opening shape, same loop ending, the
  Ashford town, and a deliberate cover (the place plus the impossible number).

| # | What's tested | Concept |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | the baseline (a human answer, bittersweet) | The Friday Pizza; The Full Shelf |
| 3–4 | **eerie answer vs human answer** | The 41st Step (eerie); Minus One Degree (human) |
| 5–6 | **"you" POV vs a told story** | 3 A.M. Roses as "your neighbour"; a school story told in the third person |
| 7–8 | **45 s vs 75 s** for the same depth of story | two new concepts |
| 9 | **a shared-town callback** (a character from #1 in the background, named in the pinned comment) | new |
| 10 | the best-performing variant again, to confirm it | new |

**Measure each video at 48 hours** (YouTube Studio):
- stayed to watch: the target is **70 % or more**; One Too Many did 85.5 %;
- average viewed: **60 % or more**;
- subscribers per 1,000 views;
- **returning viewers %** (the Audience tab);
- the share of comments that are theories or "I solved it".

**The decision after 10 videos:**
- keep the winning variables;
- if 3 or more videos beat 70 % stayed, commit to the format for the next 30;
- if none do, the problem is the execution (the hook, the pacing, the clue), not the direction. Check against One Too
  Many before changing course.
