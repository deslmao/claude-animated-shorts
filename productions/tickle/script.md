# Why you can't tickle yourself
mood: playful

- Your brain refuses to let you tickle yourself, and it's annoyingly good at it.
- Milo tries every morning. Fingers ready, armpit open, total focus.
- Nothing. Not even a giggle.
- But the second Gus sneaks up behind him, Milo laughs so hard he nearly falls over.
- gus: Works every time!
- Same fingers, same armpit. So what changed?
- Wait, is Milo secretly not ticklish?
- You see, every time you move, your brain quietly predicts what that movement will feel like.
- A part at the back of your brain, the cerebellum, sends out a forecast: fingers arriving at the armpit, any moment now.
- When the touch lands exactly as predicted, your brain turns the feeling down. No surprise, no tickle.
- It's like hearing a joke you wrote yourself. You already know the punchline.
- Scientists tested this with a tickling robot. People moved a robot arm with one hand, and it tickled their other palm.
- When the robot copied them instantly, it barely tickled. Add a tiny delay, and suddenly it did.
- The surprise is the whole trick.
- So if you ever need a guaranteed laugh, you'll need a friend.
- Because the one person on Earth who can never tickle you... is you.
