# 4,012 Steps: script (draft for the owner's approval)

**Format:** a standalone, fully narrated mystery-horror Short with an emotional twist. It's a complete story with no vote.
- **Person:** second person ("you": a teenager, never named).
- **Narrator:** `georgeHushed`, the channel's calm horror narrator from *One Too Many*.
- **No dialogue.** Sound effects carry the scary middle: claws ticking on the floorboards, the breath, the bed creaking.
- **Length:** about 271 words, about 1 min 48 s at about 150 words a minute, plus short action beats.

## Narration
| # | Line |
|---|---|
| 1 | Imagine your phone says you walked four thousand and twelve steps last night. You never got out of bed. |
| 2 | You open the map. The steps never left your room. They go round and round your bed. All night. |
| 3 | It has to be a glitch. So that night, you leave your phone charging across the room, where it can't move. |
| 4 | In the morning, it says four thousand and twelve. Again. Exactly the same. |
| 5 | You scroll back. Every night since June: four thousand and twelve. |
| 6 | So the next night, you stay awake, and you watch the counter. |
| 7 | At one fourteen, it moves. One. Two. Three. And you haven't moved at all. |
| 8 | Then you hear it. Tick, tick, tick, on the floorboards. Something small is walking round your bed. |
| 9 | Something warm breathes on your hand. |
| 10 | Then the end of the bed sinks, like something just lay down on your feet. |
| 11 | And the counter stops. Four thousand and twelve. |
| 12 | You open the app. It isn't counting your phone. It's counting Biscuit's collar. You never disconnected it. |
| 13 | Biscuit was your dog. He died in June. His collar has been in your drawer ever since. It hasn't moved. But it's still counting. |
| 14 | For nearly eleven years, he slept on the end of your bed. Four thousand and twelve nights. And every night, he walked round you first, to make sure you were safe. |
| 15 | He never stopped. That's his ghost, walking round your bed. Still guarding you while you sleep. One step for every night he kept you safe. |
| 16 | So tonight, you leave the drawer open. |
| 17 | And in the morning, your phone says four thousand and twelve steps. |

**The loop:** line 17 runs straight back into line 1 ("…four thousand and twelve steps." / "Imagine your phone says you
walked four thousand and twelve steps last night."). The film ends on the phone screen it opened on. It ends on the
line itself, with no end card and no call to action.

## Story check (one pass)
- **The number is explained, not just repeated.** 4,012 nights is 10 years 360 days, so "nearly eleven years" is
  accurate. "One step for every night he kept you safe" says why the count is exactly the same every night.
- **"Why now?" is answered by a planted clue.** Line 5 ("every night since June") means nothing on first hearing.
  Line 13 ("He died in June") pays it off, and rewarding a rewatch is a comment driver.
- **Each clue is explained when it appears:**
  - the map shows the circles (line 2);
  - the phone across the room rules out the phone (lines 3–4);
  - the app names the device (line 12).

  Viewers aren't left to decode any of it alone.
- **The impossible detail is stated outright:** the collar is in a drawer, it hasn't moved, and it's still counting
  (line 13).
- **The ghost is named outright** (line 15: "That's his ghost… still guarding you"), so no viewer is left wondering what
  was in the room or whether it meant harm. Every scary sound becomes him protecting you.
- **The ghost's body is never shown, only heard and felt:** the ticks, the breath, the bed sinking. The horror stays in the
  sound, and the reveal stays a surprise.
- **The answer is handed straight on.** After the reveal (line 12) there's no pause. Lines 13–15 explain it at once,
  then the loop. That avoids *One Too Many*'s 6% drop after its answer.
- **The time is 1:14 a.m., not 3 a.m.,** to keep it distinct from *What Ollie Says at 3 A.M.*

## Visual plan, by line
1. **The phone screen:** STEPS 4,012. Then a cut to you sitting up in bed, morning light.
2. **The map insert:** a tight scribbled circle drawn round a little bed icon.
3. **The phone on charge** on the desk across the room; a wide shot of the room.
4. **Morning:** the phone, 4,012.
5. **The history insert:** a column of nights, every one 4,012, scrolling back to a month marked JUNE.
6. **Night:** you awake in bed, the phone glowing in your hand.
7. **The counter insert:** 1:14, then 0 → 1 → 2 → 3, one count per tick. Then you, frozen.
8. **Low across the floorboards** to the bed (nothing there); claw ticks circling in the sound.
9. **Your hand** hanging over the edge of the bed; the hairs lift (insert).
10. **The foot of the duvet** dips under nothing (insert); a creak.
11. **The counter:** 4,012. It stops.
12. **The app's device list:** BISCUIT'S COLLAR · CONNECTED.
13. **The drawer opens:** a red collar with a bone tag, BISCUIT. A framed photo of the dog on the bedside table.
14. **The photo, closer:** the dog curled on the end of this bed.
15. **Faint glowing paw prints** appear one by one on the floorboards, in a circle round your bed. Then you, a tear,
    half a smile. The ghost is shown only as its paw prints.
16. **Your hand** leaves the drawer open; the lamp goes off.
17. **Morning:** the phone, 4,012, exactly the opening frame.
