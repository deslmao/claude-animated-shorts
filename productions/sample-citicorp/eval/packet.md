# Research: the Citicorp Center engineering crisis (1978)

## Summary
In 1978 the structural engineer William LeMessurier concluded that the new Citicorp Center (Manhattan, 59 storeys,
completed 1977, standing on four stilts at the midpoints of its sides because of St. Peter's Lutheran Church) was
vulnerable to quartering (corner) winds. The danger was worsened by a change from welded to bolted brace joints. Citicorp
funded secret night-time repairs: steel plates welded over the bolted joints. Evacuation plans were drawn up but not
publicised. Hurricane Ella threatened the city while the repairs were half finished, then turned out to sea. The repairs were
completed in October 1978, and a newspaper strike helped keep the story out of the press until a 1995 New Yorker article.
A NIST reassessment (Duthinh, 2019–2020) concluded that the quartering-wind loads were not the threat they had been believed to be.

## Facts used (with sources)
- **The tower stands on four nine-storey stilts at the midpoints of its sides, because the church site occupied the corner**, with a cantilever over the new church. [PBS Building Big](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/buildingbig/wonder/structure/citicorp.html); [RT Specialty](https://rtspecialty.com/ecp/the-design-flaw-that-almost-wiped-out-an-nyc-skyscraper/)
- **A tuned mass damper, a block of about 400 tons, sits at the top to reduce sway, and it needs power to run.** Sources give 370–410 tons, so the script says "a huge concrete block" and no figure. [Engineers Ireland](https://www.engineersireland.ie/Engineers-Journal/Civil/citicorp-center-tower-how-failure-was-averted); [99% Invisible](https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/structural-integrity/)
- **Diane Hartley, a Princeton engineering undergraduate**, asked about quartering winds for her thesis. Accounts differ on whether she spoke to LeMessurier or to a junior engineer at his firm. The script says "called his office" and shows a junior engineer answering. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citicorp_Center_engineering_crisis); [99% Invisible](https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/structural-integrity/)
- **The welded joints of the braces were changed to bolted joints** to save costs, a change LeMessurier learned of afterwards. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citicorp_Center_engineering_crisis); [Online Ethics Center, "The Fifty-Nine-Story Crisis"](https://onlineethics.org/node/38356)
- **LeMessurier's calculation:** quartering winds raised the load at the bolted joints by about 160 %. Without the damper (a power failure), a storm strong enough to topple the tower could occur about once every 16 years, against about 55 years with the damper running. The script attributes this to "his own maths". [CNN/CTV, "1 in 16 chance"](https://www.ctvnews.ca/sci-tech/article/this-new-york-skyscraper-had-a-1-in-16-chance-of-collapse-only-one-man-knew/); [99% Invisible](https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/structural-integrity/)
- **He informed the owners; repairs welded 2-inch steel plates over about 200 bolted joints, at night, over about three months, finished in October 1978.** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citicorp_Center_engineering_crisis); [Online Ethics Center](https://onlineethics.org/node/38356)
- **Evacuation plans** for the surrounding area were drawn up but not publicised. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citicorp_Center_engineering_crisis)
- **Hurricane Ella** threatened New York while the repairs were about half done, then turned out to sea. [RT Specialty](https://rtspecialty.com/?p=11876); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citicorp_Center_engineering_crisis)
- **A New York newspaper strike** at the time kept the story out of the press. It became public in Joe Morgenstern's 1995 New Yorker article. [Online Ethics Center](https://onlineethics.org/node/38356); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citicorp_Center_engineering_crisis)
- **NIST reassessment:** "if the building had been safely designed for head-on winds, then it would have been safe for quartering winds", and face winds produced more structural demand than corner winds. [NIST, "Blown Away: revisiting a famous engineering case"](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/taking-measure/blown-away-revisiting-famous-engineering-case); [NIST publication](https://www.nist.gov/publications/blown-away-citicorp-center-tower-repairs-revisited)
