**Description:**
```
Some of history's most feared pirates ran their ships like tiny democracies. 🏴‍☠️

In the early 1700s, many pirate crews signed written articles before a voyage. The crew elected its captain and could vote him out, an elected quartermaster shared out the food and loot, and men injured in battle were paid from a common fund. Economist Peter Leeson argues these rules were how a ship of outlaws could work without owners on land. The rules varied from ship to ship, and pirates were still violent criminals, but some crews had injury pay long before most countries did.

Sources: Peter Leeson, "An-arrgh-chy: The Law and Economics of Pirate Organization" (Journal of Political Economy, 2007); Bartholomew Roberts' articles (1720s).
```
