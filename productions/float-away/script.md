# Thinkora Video 08: "In This World, If Nobody Thinks About You, You Float Away". Script v1

**Format:** story-first emotional short with comedy beats, about 82 s, Milo narrating in the first person.

**Status:** stages A–D done. Nothing has been generated or rendered yet. The next step is your approval of the script.

---

## Stage A: Concept

**Title:** In This World, If Nobody Thinks About You, You Float Away

**Premise:** In this world, the more people think about you, the heavier you get. When Milo's lonely grandpa starts
floating away, Milo has to make the whole world think about him before he drifts off for good.

**Why it works:**
- **The rule is clear in one sentence and gives instant visual comedy:** movie stars snap chairs, and politicians
  sink into the pavement on election day.
- **It turns an everyday feeling (being forgotten) into something you can see.** Weight stands for being cared
  about.
- **It has a built-in emotional trap.** We blame "the street" for forgetting Grandpa, then learn the truth.
- **The solution uses the rule cleverly:** one kid's memories aren't enough, so he goes live to 40,000 people.
- **The ending line is a call to action people will share.**

**Main character:** Milo is a good kid who drifted away from his grandpa without noticing: a new school, a new
phone. Motivation: get Grandpa back down. What he stands to lose: the last family member who is waiting for him.
His decision: run into a storm, then turn himself into a public spectacle to save him.

**Central conflict:** Grandpa Gus is losing weight because nobody thinks about him. A storm finally carries him
off, and one person's memories can't pull him back down.

**Twist and payoff:**
1. **Mid-story reveal:** his pockets clink. He has been filling them with stones to hide how light he has
   become.
2. **Main reversal:** "He started floating the spring I stopped visiting. I was the last one." It wasn't the street
   that forgot him. It was Milo.
3. **Payoff:** the livestream, then the crash that cracks the road, then the callback to the chair-breaking movie
   stars.
4. **Final line:** a call to action that hits harder because of the story: "He might be holding onto the
   counter."

*Concepts rejected internally:* hearing your name whenever it's said (too audio-only to animate well); shadows
that act on what you really want (not feasible without new rig work); lifespan or age signs (too dark, and done
often).

| 19 | Milo | He came down so hard, he cracked the road. |
| 20 | Milo | Now he breaks every chair he sits on. He blames me. |
| 21 | Milo | So call your grandpa. Right now. He might be holding onto the counter. |

**Length:** 209 words, about 1,150 characters. At the measured pace of our voices (about 2.8 words a second), that
is about 75 s of speech. With the planned pauses and visual holds it comes to about **82 s**, inside the 65–85 s
target.

**Quality check against your ten questions:**
1. **Hook in 2 seconds:** the rule plus a movie star's chair snapping.
2. **Every section adds something:** the rule, three escalating examples, Grandpa, the decline, the guilt set-up,
   the stones, the storm, a failed rescue, the reversal, the plan, the payoff, the callback, the call to action.
3. **Continuous development:** no line repeats information.
4. **Clear conflict:** Grandpa is floating away and one person isn't enough.
5. **Reason to care:** he hid the stones so Milo wouldn't worry; Milo's guilt is relatable.
6. **Surprising twist:** "I was the last one" flips who was to blame. Line 8 plants it without giving it away.
7. **Satisfying ending:** a physical payoff (the cracked road), a comic callback, then an emotional sting.
8. **Visuals carry the story:** every rule beat is a visible event: snapping, sinking, floating, the clinking
   stones, the counter, the view counter, the crash.
9. **Nothing to cut:** each line was tested for removal. Line 15 is short but needed: the attempt fails.
10. **Rewatchable:** yes. Line 8 reads differently once you know the twist.
