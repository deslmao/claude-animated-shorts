# Locked Out
spec: 3
format: mystery
perspective: first
why: A first-person mystery told through a locksmith's hands and trade knowledge: the viewer checks each piece of evidence with me (an ID, a dog, a drawing, a lock that's too new), is addressed by the man at the door, and reaches the decision at the same moment I do.
length: 95
voice: liamQuiet
voices: me=liamQuiet, hale=brian, operator=alice, woman=jessica
mood: tense
factual: no

## Premise
- question: At 2:14 a.m. a man is paying me double to open his own front door, quietly. Is it really his door, and why has his key stopped working?
- protagonist: me, a night-call locksmith on a final written warning
- want: to do the job right, and keep the job
- fear: being wrong either way: opening a home to the wrong man, or leaving an honest man out in the cold again
- protects: whoever is behind the door
- decision: to go back to the door and jam the lock for good, instead of opening it or driving away
- loses: my job, if I wrongly refuse him (shown: the final warning on my van's visor); someone's safety, if I wrongly open
- obstacle: every check he passes is genuine. The truth isn't a lie he tells; it's something he leaves out
- rule: none
- conflict: A, he's an honest man locked out of his own home after a row; B, he's not meant to be let in, and I'm the way in
- action: none

## Spine
depth: deep

### Constraints
- Kestrel Locks is the only 24-hour locksmith in town. At night, jobs are booked automatically through its app: no dispatcher is awake. Earlier jobs at an address sit one tap deeper, under History.
- Company rule: proof of address before I touch a lock. A driving licence counts.
- Last winter I refused a man who had no ID. It was his house, and his complaint was upheld. The final warning hangs on my van's visor: one more means my job.
- A driving licence keeps its old address until its holder changes it.
- The lock on number 9 is a new cylinder with a thumb-turn inside. A pick broken off in its keyway blocks it from outside only.
- The street is asleep. A locksmith opening a door looks legitimate; breaking glass doesn't.
- At night the police are about four minutes away.
- My stopwatch is on my wrist, under my cuff. My phone fits in my jacket's breast pocket.
- Kestrel's cylinders carry a small KL stamp on their face.

- chosen: 1. Its hook carries situation, stake and question in one line; its reveal turns the evidence itself around (the proof that he belongs is the danger); and first person is what makes it work: my hands and my trade knowledge are the plot, and the climax is a decision made with my own tools. Direction 2 is guessable early and confined to a car; direction 3 is gentle, with low stakes.
