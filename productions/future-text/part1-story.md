# "I Got a Text From Myself 10 Minutes in the Future": Part 1 (story for approval)

**Status:** story only. No narration, assets, storyboard or render yet. Parts 2–5 are not written or planned.
**Working story id:** `future-text`. **Target length:** about 85–90 s (167 spoken words plus long action beats).

---

## 1. Core story and premise

**The premise in one image:** Milo's phone buzzes, and the sender is **his own number**, with his own selfie as the
contact photo. The clock at the top of the screen says **8:02**, but the message is stamped **8:12**.
*A text from himself, ten minutes from now.* The first 10 seconds show it, say it once, and give it time to land.

**Protagonist: Milo** (our existing lead, a young teen)
- **Personality:** always late, quick to joke, quicker to panic, too proud to admit he's scared, good-hearted underneath.
- **His arc in Part 1:**
  - He laughs it off as a prank.
  - He gets rattled when a small prediction comes true.
  - He's shaken when the second one nearly hurts him.
  - Finally he accepts "it's actually me".

**Goal (clear and simple):** today is his **maths final**, and the **8:05 bus** is the only way to get there on time.

**Conflict:** the only person he can't argue with, himself, tells him **not to get on that bus**, and won't say why.

**Shape of the episode:**
1. Hook.
2. Disbelief.
3. A small proof (funny).
4. A big proof (dangerous).
5. A hard decision: let the bus go.
6. A race against the clock to school.
7. A proof that it really is him (funny, personal).
8. He makes it: the mini goal is won.
9. **The twist:** the bus arrives, perfectly fine, and **another Milo steps off it**.
10. One last text from the future: **"Don't let him see you."**
11. A/B vote.

**The one mystery we leave open:** who is the Milo who got on the bus, and why is future-Milo afraid of him?
Nothing else is left unexplained.

**The rule, shown consistently:** every message from "Me" is stamped exactly 10 minutes ahead of the phone's clock,
and every prediction comes true. Viewers can check it on screen; the narration only states it once.

## 3. Scene-by-scene visual breakdown

Text messages appear as **chat bubbles floating beside Milo in the scene**, readable and facing the camera, so we never cut away
from the action to read them. There are only **two phone close-ups**, both at moments of discovery: the first message with its
timestamp, and the password reveal.

### Scene 1: The hook (0:00–0:05), city street, bright morning
- **Shot:** a low tracking shot as Milo sprints round the corner.
  - Toast in his mouth, backpack on one shoulder, a shoelace flapping.
  - The bus stop is ahead.
- **Action:** his pocket buzzes mid-stride. He skids to a stop and pulls out the phone.
- **Line 1:** "At 8:02, Milo got a text from his own number."
- **Cut, POV insert (phone close-up 1):**
  - The contact is **"Me"**, with Milo's own goofy selfie.
  - The message bubble lands with a thump: **"Whatever you do, DON'T get on the bus."**
  - Line 2 is heard in Milo's own phone-filtered voice.

### Scene 2: The premise lands (0:05–0:11)
- **Same insert:** a slow push from the message down to its timestamp, **8:12**, then up to the phone's clock, **8:02**.
  - Each number gets a soft "tick".
  - A one-beat hold of silence.
- **Line 3:** "It was sent at 8:12. Ten minutes from now."
- **Cut:** a close-up of Milo. Narrowed eyes, then a smirk.

### Scene 3: Disbelief and the stakes (0:11–0:19), the bus stop
- **Action:** Milo snorts and pockets the phone. **Line 4:** "Ha. Nice try, Theo."
- **Shot:** a quick pan up the bus-stop sign: **"8:05 – HILLCREST SCHOOL"**.
- **Action:** Milo checks his watch, mimes "come on, come on", then hops up and down.
- **Line 5:** the stakes.
- **Background:** the bus appears small at the far end of the street, heading his way.

### Scene 4: A small proof, played as comedy (0:19–0:30)
- **Action:**
  - Milo thumbs a reply. A bubble pops up beside him: **"Who is this?"** (line 6).
  - The reply bubble bursts in at once: **"You. Want proof? Your left shoelace is about to snap."** (line 7)
- **Shot:** a close-up of his trainer. He rolls his eyes and takes one step… **SNAP**.
- **Physical gag:**
  - He stumbles into the bin, and the toast flips out of his mouth and lands jam-side down.
  - A pigeon waddles in and takes it.
- **Line 8:** a two-shot of Milo and the pigeon, his face frozen. "…Okay. Lucky guess."

### Scene 5: A big proof, played as danger (0:30–0:41)
- **Action:** the bus pulls in with a hiss, and the doors fold open.
  - A low angle from behind Milo shows the driver above him.
- **Line 9 (DRIVER):** "You getting on, kid?"
- **The text:** Milo lifts a foot onto the step. Buzz. A red bubble: **"Look left. Now."** (line 10)
- **Shot:** a whip pan left.
  - A **red car runs the red light** and blasts through the gutter puddle.
  - A wall of water drenches the bench and the spot by the kerb where Milo was standing a second ago. He'd stepped back to read.
  - The music cuts out. The water drips.
- **Line 11:** Milo stares at the soaked bench. "That… would've been me."

### Scene 6: The decision; let it breathe (0:41–0:50)
- **Action:**
  - The driver taps the wheel: "Last chance!" (line 12).
  - Milo looks at the bus, then the phone, then the bus. "I'll… walk." (line 13)
- **Shot:** the doors close. A **long held wide** from behind Milo as the bus pulls away down the street, getting smaller.
  - Milo stands alone at the stop in wet trainers.
- **Line 14:** "The only bus to school left without him."

### Scene 7: The race (0:50–1:00)
- **The text:** buzz. A bubble: **"Good. Now run. Six minutes."** (line 15)
- **Montage:** energetic tracking shots, each one a different obstacle:
  - Milo vaults the bench.
  - He sprints across the crossing as the green man starts flashing.
  - He squeezes between a parked car and a lamp post.
- **Mid-run:** he shouts at his phone (line 16), and the reply bubble trails beside him as he runs (line 17).

### Scene 8: Proof that it's him (1:00–1:09)
- **Action:**
  - Milo stops at a corner, hands on his knees, gasping, and narrows his eyes at the phone (line 18).
  - **POV insert (phone close-up 2):** the reply types itself: **"pancakes123. Change it."** (line 19)
- **Reaction:**
  - Milo's face goes from suspicious to mortified.
  - He glances around as if someone might have heard, then hugs the phone to his chest. "…It's actually me." (line 20)
- **This is the moment he stops doubting.**

### Scene 9: Goal won (1:09–1:15), the school gate
- **Shot:** a low hero shot.
  - Milo bursts through the school gate (a **"HILLCREST SCHOOL"** sign) and skids to a stop.
  - The big school clock reads **8:14**.
- **Line 21:** "He made it with one minute to spare."
- **Action:** he punches the air, laughing with relief.

### Scene 10: The twist (1:15–1:25)
- **Line 22:** the familiar hiss. The **same 8:05 bus** pulls up at the stop outside the gate: late, not a scratch on it.
  - Laughing students spill out.
- **Line 23:** Milo, offended at the universe: "I ran for nothing?!"
- **Line 24:** music drops to a single low note. The doors stay open.
- **The reveal:** one last pair of white trainers steps down, then a **mustard hoodie** and **spiky brown hair**.
  - **Another Milo** steps off the bus.
  - **Contrast, visual only (a rewatch detail):** his hoodie is dry, both laces are tied, and **he's eating toast**.
- **Shot:**
  - A slow push in on our Milo's face, his jaw dropping.
  - Then an over-the-shoulder shot from behind our Milo: the other Milo stands across the road, chewing.
- **Line 25 (a whisper):** "…That's me."

### Scene 11: The cliffhanger and the vote (1:25–1:30)
- **The last text:** buzz. The last bubble, stamped **8:24**: **"Don't let him see you."** (line 26)
- **Action:** across the road, the other Milo **slowly turns his head** toward our Milo.
- **The freeze:** right before their eyes meet, the frame freezes, desaturates, and the A/B cards slide in:
  - **A: TALK TO HIM**
  - **B: HIDE & FOLLOW HIM**
- **Line 27:** the question. The school bell rings; the exam is starting.

## 4. The A/B decision

**"Should Milo talk to him… or hide and follow him?"**
- **A: TALK TO HIM**
- **B: HIDE & FOLLOW HIM**

The bell ringing over the vote adds a cost to both options: whichever he chooses, the exam he ran for is starting without him.

---

## 5. How both choices lead to a strong Part 2 (no storyline is locked in)

- **A: Talk to him.**
  - **What it gives Part 2:** a face-to-face scene with a double, which is our strongest character-interaction setup yet.
    - The fun of Milo vs. Milo: the same jokes and the same habits, but something slightly *off*.
    - Future-Milo's texts can push back in real time ("Don't tell him about the texts").
    - That gives a three-way tension: Milo, the double, and the voice from ten minutes ahead.
- **B: Hide and follow him.**
  - **What it gives Part 2:** a stealth and suspense episode with new settings.
    - Milo shadows his double through the school and beyond, and watches him do things Milo would never do.
    - That turns the double into a visual mystery, with discoveries shown rather than explained.
    - The texts become a guide or a warning system ("He's about to turn around").

Both keep the same open questions: who the double is, and why future-Milo fears him. Both work whatever we
later decide those answers are. Part 2 will be written only after we see the winning vote.
