# Phase-1 issues: Locked Out
Each must appear in story.md's ## Issue log with a resolution and a status (resolved / no longer applies: reason).

- **I1** (cause and effect, weak): The title act, jamming the lock, does not cause the rescue. The window was always available (he was already at it in E8), so the snap closes a route the man had already shown he would abandon, and the outcome is delivered by the four-minute schedule, not by the protagonist's choice. What does protect the family is the stall in E9 and E10; the climax itself is a gesture. A viewer will feel the timing rather than the decision, and a commenter will say "he had a brick the whole time".
  - suggested fix: Make the snap buy the last stretch, visibly, on the timer. Snap at about 02:40, straight after "Open it. Now.", then spend the remaining minute on the consequence: he rams his own key at the blocked keyway, claws at the stub, orders me to get it out, and only then goes for the brick as the street turns blue. And let E6 give evidence, not orders (see plot hole), so E7 is his own decision.
- **I2** (plot hole, weak): (1) The app. The firm's own record for this address says "Do NOT open for him. Call police", yet the same app booked a lockout there the next night and showed none of it on the job card. The rule that covers this (no dispatcher at night, History one tap deeper) is in the rules list but not in any event, so the viewer never learns it, and the whole plot hangs on it. Top-comment probe: "The app literally said DO NOT OPEN and still sent him there." (2) The phone. He leaves a live 999 call face-up in an open bag at the door while the man stands over him (E9, E10). The man also had his shadow across the History screen in E6 and could see my lit face on a call through the windscreen in E8. Three chances to see it, no reaction; the device that gives the viewer the countdown is in the antagonist's plain view. (3) Simpler means. Locked in the van, the horn would wake a street the man needs asleep. The story has an answer (while he believes the quiet way still works he puts the brick down, which E9 shows), but nothing shows the protagonist weighing it. (4) "No paperwork" (E1) for a job he booked through an app that has already made a record. (5) The jammed lock leaves the window, covered under cause and effect.
  - suggested fix: (1) Make the History entry a fact, not a flag: "Yesterday. Emergency lock change. Customer: R. Hale. Keys to R. Hale only." He and the viewer put it together with the dead key, the new cylinder and the light, and the operator's line in E8 confirms it. That removes the hole and keeps the job at risk until E8. (2) Either hide the phone (screen-down under the bag flap, with the timer shown to the viewer only) or use it: he sees the 999 screen, and that is what turns "You said one minute" into the hand on the shoulder. (3) One image in E8: my hand over the horn, him raising the brick at the glass, my hand coming away. (4) Have him ask for "no invoice, no call to the office" instead.
- **I3** (consistency, weak): (1) E7 says "Everything he told me was true", and the premise says the truth "isn't a lie he tells". But in E3 he says "They're at her mother's", a plain lie that the light in E5 exposes. The story's own best idea and its own declared Tower 9 technique contradict each other. (2) The fitter's sticker is "on the door's edge beside the lock". The edge of a shut door is inside the frame and cannot be seen or thumbed; and my torch went over that lock closely in E4 without finding a sticker from my own firm. (3) The upstairs light enters the objects table only at E5, already going out. It must have been on since E1, on a street of "dark windows", beside a man saying nobody is home, and nobody noticed. (4) The thumb-turn is not in the objects table and is never seen, though E13 depends on it. (5) The licence goes into my gloved hand in E2 and is never tracked again. (6) The tool bag that holds the phone from E9 is not tracked: it has to come from the van in E9, which works, but should be shown. Timer arithmetic (00:48, 01:50, 02:40, 03:51 against "four minutes out") and the brick's three positions are consistent.
  - suggested fix: (1) Change his E3 line to something true and evasive ("She won't come down. Not at this hour.") or drop the claim that everything was true. (2) Put the sticker on the face of the new cylinder's collar or on the letterbox flap, found only when I lean in at E6. (3) Plant the strip of light in the E1 wide shot, unremarked, so E5 is a detail the viewer could have checked. (4) Show her hand on the thumb-turn in E13. (5) Have him take the licence back in E2, or hand it to an officer in E12.
- **I4** (emotional cost, weak): The cost on his side is the job: one more upheld complaint. It is shown before the climax (E1, E2) and it does real work once, when he accepts the ID in E2. Then E6 cancels it: with a written "do not open, call police" from his own firm, no refusal can be a wrong refusal. From E6 to E13 doing the right thing costs him one pick. What remains at the climax is physical risk (a hand on his shoulder, a shove), which is real but was never the stake the story set up, and the cost to the family is carried by people unseen until E13. The letter is then covered in E14 without ever having been in danger.
  - suggested fix: Keep the job at risk when he chooses. With the E6 change above he walks to the van on a judgement, not an instruction, with the visor letter in front of him as he dials (E8): if he is wrong, this is the second complaint. Optionally let the man say it at E10 ("I'll have your job for this"), so the snap in E11 is made knowing the price.
- **I5** (reproduction, weak): Closest sources are Tower 9 and The Last Stop. Sequence: different (they sit inside and are approached; here the protagonist stands outside with the suspect and is asked to do the opening). Motivations: different (a tradesman's rule and his job against a father who wants back in). Central conflict: different (not "which voice is lying" but "every proof is real and still wrong"). Solution: its own (stall, then destroy his own ability to open the door). Meaning of the payoff: its own ("You didn't open it"; the job's rule justified). No sequence, scene or line is reproduced. But one listed distinctive element is: the benchmarks name "Mr. Hale" as distinctive expression of The Last Stop, not to be reproduced, and this story, which declares The Last Stop as an inspiration, calls its ambiguous man D. HALE and puts HALE on the bell and in the app. The 2:07 a.m. start also repeats the benchmarks' 4:07 and 3:07 to the minute digit.
  - suggested fix: Rename the family (any surname not in the benchmarks) and move the time off ":07".
