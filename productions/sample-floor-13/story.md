# Floor 13
format: immersive+mystery
why: The viewer is trapped with Milo and has to choose between two voices that each have evidence, so second person plus competing explanations is the engine; the knock is a clue that a later reveal recontextualises.
length: 70
voice: georgeHushed
voices: milo=liam, mum=jessica, intercom=brian
mood: tense
factual: no

## Premise
- question: Who is really outside the lift doors on a floor that shouldn't exist?
- protagonist: Milo (the viewer, in the second person)
- want: to get home to his mum
- fear: opening the doors to the wrong person
- protects: himself, and his mum if she really is out there
- decision: open the doors for the voice that knows the family knock, or obey the intercom
- rule: none
- conflict: The intercom says what's outside is not his mum and the doors must stay shut; Mum's voice says she's on floor 13 and needs him to open them
- action: none

## Setups and payoffs
| setup | beat | payoff beat | how it pays off |
|---|---|---|---|
| The family knock (two quick taps, one slow) that Milo does on his own front door | 1 | 7 | the knock on the lift doors is exactly that pattern, which is strong evidence for Mum |
| The lift's camera screen showing the corridor | 3 | 9 | the screen shows Mum's coat on the floor of the empty corridor, so the evidence swings back |

## Ending
- type: decision
- A: OPEN THE DOORS -> if it is Mum, he saves her from whatever took her coat; if not, he lets it in
- B: TRUST THE INTERCOM -> he stays safe in the lift, but if that's Mum, he leaves her out there alone

## Evaluation
- verdict: ready
- weaknesses: (1) In v1 the knock came before the viewer had seen the family knock, so it meant nothing. (2) v1 explained the intercom's authority with a narrator line instead of letting Milo see the corridor. (3) Beats 5 and 6 both showed Milo doubting without new evidence.
- revision: The family knock is now planted in beat 1 as Milo's habit. Beat 3 gives the intercom its camera screen, so its claims have something visible behind them. Beat 6 now brings new information (no signal: he can't check either story), and the coat in beat 9 gives the screen a second, contradictory piece of evidence.
- recheck: The evidence alternates Mum, intercom, Mum, intercom, unresolved, and every swing is something the viewer sees or hears. The decision is understandable, and each option has a stated risk. Remaining risk: three voices in 80 s; the intercom's flat electronic voice keeps them distinct.
