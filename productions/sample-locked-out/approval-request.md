# Locked Out: story approval

**Format:** mystery. A first-person mystery told through a locksmith's hands and trade knowledge: the viewer checks each piece of evidence with me (an ID, a dog, a drawing, a lock that's too new), is addressed by the man at the door, and reaches the decision at the same moment I do.

**Estimated length:** 87 s · **Words:** 263 · **Narration:** 1335 characters (about 1335 ElevenLabs credits) · **Voices:** narrator=liamQuiet, marsh=brian, me=liam, operator=alice, neighbour=chris, woman=jessica

## Premise
- **question:** At 2:14 a.m. a man is paying me double to open his own front door, quietly. Is it really his door, and why has his key stopped working?
- **protagonist:** me, a night-call locksmith on a final written warning
- **want:** to do the job right, and keep the job
- **fear:** being wrong either way: opening a home to the wrong man, or leaving an honest man out in the cold again
- **protects:** whoever is behind the door
- **decision:** to go back and keep him at the front door until the police come; then, when he demands I open it, to refuse him out loud, where the whole street can hear
- **loses:** my job, if I refuse a man with valid ID (shown: the final warning on my van's visor, and the refusal logged at the end); her and her son's safety, if I open
- **obstacle:** every check he passes is genuine. The truth isn't a lie he tells; it's something he leaves out
- **conflict:** A, he's an honest man locked out of his own home after a row; B, he's not meant to be let in, and I'm the way in

## Independent evaluation
**Verdict:** ready → ready · **Would a viewer keep watching?** yes. The opening line is a genuine hook (a man paying double to break into his own house). Viewers will be gripped at the dog's tail thumping at "Biscuit. It's me" beside the DADDY drawing (5), the light snapping off as the tools come out (7), the History reveal "one of ours" (8), and the brick weighed at the glass with the narrator's hand over the horn (11). Swipe risks: the procedural stretch of beats 3–4 (licence, bell) if it's paced slowly; the static decision in beat 12; and the quiet coda right after the arrest (17), where some will leave before the best line. Keep beats 18–19 tight. Top comment: "'I checked who he was. I never asked who the lock was for.' That line." (A runner-up: "The dog wagging at his voice is the saddest part.") Top critical comment: "He said honking would make him smash the window and then screamed down the entire street?" A runner-up is "Why did he call the same company that changed the locks?"
