# Flat Again
spec: 3
format: immersive+mystery
why: The viewer is the victim of a small, strange, repeated act (tyres let down, caps left in a neat row), investigates it against a deadline, weighs two kinds of motive with clues they can check (a padlock, a key, a habit, a gesture), and catches the culprit in the act; the reveal turns every act of sabotage into an act of protection.
length: 90
voice: georgeHushed
voices: you=liam, grandad=brian, mum=jessica, dex=chris, shah=georgeLively
mood: tense
factual: no

## Premise
- question: Someone keeps letting your bike tyres down before dawn, and leaves the valve caps standing in a neat little row. Who is doing it, and why?
- protagonist: you (the viewer), thirteen, with a paper round, living with Mum and Grandad Ray
- want: to keep the paper round: three late starts and it goes to Dex
- fear: that someone is out to get you, and that it might be someone at home
- protects: your round, and your pride
- decision: to sit up in the dark and catch whoever it is in the act, instead of hiding the bike
- loses: the round (shown: Mr. Shah counting your lates on his fingers); and, without knowing it, your safety on Gallows Hill
- obstacle: whoever does it has a key, and the one person who knows why can't say it
- rule: none
- conflict: A, someone wants you to lose the round (Dex, who wants it, or Mum, who thinks it's wrecking your sleep); B, someone is trying to keep you off the bike, and can't tell you why
- action: none

## Spine
depth: deep

### Constraints
- Mr. Shah's paper round starts at 6:00, and the ride takes about twenty minutes. He chalks a tally mark on the shop door for every late start: three, and the round goes to Dex, who wants it.
- The round pays for your school trip (a jar on the kitchen shelf: TRIP, nearly full).
- The only way to the shop in time is Gallows Hill: steep, and it ends at a junction with the ring road, where lorries pass.
- Grandad Ray had a stroke in the spring. His words come out wrong or not at all, he can't write, his right hand can't grip, and he walks slowly with a stick.
- He mended bikes for forty years. The shed was his workshop, his old parts box (RAY'S CYCLES) is still on the shelf, and he built your bike: it wears his RAY'S CYCLES badge. A photo on the shed wall shows him holding the seat of your first bike and tapping two fingers on your little helmet.
- Mum leaves for her bakery shift at 4:30 a.m. Her car passes the shed.
- From Tuesday the shed has a padlock and two keys: yours, and the household spare on the hook by the back door, next to Grandad's room. The shed window is painted shut.
- A brake cable frays inside the lever. It keeps working until the last strands go.
- Letting a tyre down takes one thumb on the valve.

- chosen: 1. Its evidence is physical and checkable by the viewer (a locked padlock, two keys, a neat habit, a gesture), it has two real kinds of motive to weigh, and its reveal changes the meaning of every earlier act (sabotage becomes protection). The protagonist's flaw (not listening to Grandad) is the reason the mystery exists, so the payoff changes them. Direction 2 has the harder ending but a weaker chain of evidence; direction 3 is a judgement, not an investigation.
