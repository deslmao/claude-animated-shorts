# Critic report, round 1: "GTA 6, Explained to Your Girlfriend · Ep 1"

I read all 20 full-resolution frames, the motion strip and both reference sheets (35 REF frames).

## Scores

| Category | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Characters | 4 | The designs are readable and on-model in the flat, but the poses barely change: arms hang at the sides or are crossed in almost every frame. Mouth shapes are the only acting. Gordon is off-model in f14 (a plain orange shirt with no palm print or white tee, and round shades). In the game world the characters are blurred (f15, f16, f17) and look pasted on, with no contact shadow except in f14. The REF characters hold props, lean, gesture and stand with their feet planted on lit ground. |
| Motion | 3 | In the motion strip, frames 0-15 and 1395-1407 are almost identical: no body motion, no camera move, only the caption changes. In 1140-1155 Gordon simply walks out of the frame past a locked camera. The pose variety across all 20 frames is very low. |
| Light | 4 | The night-flat grade is muddy and crushes the faces (f6, f7, f9, f18, f20). Lucy's face is a dark-brown blob against black hair and a navy wall. No TV glow or rim light reads on the characters, although the TV is meant to light the scene. The game world has good sun, but the 2D characters don't take its light or colour (f15-f17 look flat and pasted on). The REF uses warm and cool motivated key lights, rim light on the characters, and practicals such as a torch, candle or lantern in almost every shot. |
| World | 4 | 8 of the 20 frames (2, 3, 4, 8, 10, 11, 13, 19) are full-screen flat graphics with no world around them. The REF always places an insert inside a set: the $100 bill on a wooden table, the sign on a wall, the monitors in a room. The real-life rooms are sparse: a bed and a wall, a sofa, a lamp. The game city is the strongest part (f14, f16), but it uses blocky, primitive cars, an empty pink sky over half of f17, and a featureless sea and sky in f15. |
| Camera | 4 | The shots are dominated by repeated centred medium close-ups (f1, f5, f9, f12, f18, f20). Heads are cut by the frame edge in f12, f16 and f18, and in the walk shot at 1140-1155. f6 and f7 are the same two-shot repeated. There are no wides of the real rooms, no low or high angles and no over-the-shoulder shots. The REF varies wide shots, POV, overheads and deep-staged two-shots. |
| Captions | 5 | The captions are legible, consistently placed (y ≈ 1235, or y ≈ 580 in the game) and use the speaker colours. But they sit on Lucy's lower face in f9. The top banner's text overflows its box (f1, f2), and the banner covers the phone clock in f2 and at 1461-1470, where the clock carries the payoff joke. The caption is small for a 1080-wide frame compared with the REF. |

## Per-frame notes

- **f01 (0):** Lucy medium close-up in the bedroom; REF 4_26. The REF puts a prop in hand and shows the room in depth. Ours is a static, centred figure against a blank wall, and the banner overflows its box.
- **f02 (40):** Phone typing "hey 🙂"; REF 4_42 (screens in a room). The REF frames its screen inside a lit set. Ours is a full-screen flat UI, with the banner over the clock.
- **f03 (110):** Phone, "sorry. gta"; REF 4_42. The tilt is a nice touch, but the UI elements are cropped at the edges.
- **f04 (170):** Title card; REF 5_10 ("NO FLUSHING TONIGHT" sign). The REF's sign is in a set with characters. Ours is a flat graphic with an empty bottom third.
- **f05 (215):** Gordon medium close-up, night; REF 3_50. A good expression, but a dark flat background with no detail, and the face is under-lit.
- **f06 (300):** Two-shot in the flat; REF 3_42. Decent staging and the controller prop works. But the grade is murky and the TV doesn't light anyone.
- **f07 (330):** A near-duplicate of f06; REF 3_42. It adds no new angle.
- **f08 (420):** Calendar insert; REF 3_18 ($100 bill on a table). The REF's insert sits on a textured surface with light. Ours is a flat calendar on a lined backdrop.
- **f09 (525):** Lucy close-up, smug; REF 3_50. A strong expression, but the caption sits on her chin and her face is very dark.
- **f10 (700):** View counter; REF 5_10. Text collision and edge crops.
- **f11 (800):** DELAYED poster; REF 5_10. Readable and punchy. The stamp hides "MAY 2026" (acceptable if intended), and the poster is flat with no environment.

- **f13 (1000):** Receipt; REF 3_18. Legible, but the dark, empty surround makes it a flat graphic.

## Top fixes (weakest category first)

1. **Motion and characters: make every character shot move.** In f01, f05, f06, f09, f12, f18 and f20 (and the 0-15 and 1395-1407 stretches), add a slow push-in or dolly on every shot (at least 5-8% scale over the shot). Give each speaker a gesture keyed to the line: Lucy points at the phone in f01, Gordon leans and works the controller in f05 and f12, Lucy uncrosses her arms and grabs the controller in f20. Re-cut f07 as a new angle (an over-the-shoulder from behind the sofa toward the TV) instead of repeating f06. Put Gordon back on-model in f14 (palm-print shirt, white tee, rectangular shades).
2. **Camera and captions: clear the edges and the faces.** Reframe f12, f16 and f18 so both heads sit at least 60 px inside the frame. Keep the walker in frame in 1140-1155 with a pan, or end the shot before he exits. Lift f09's caption below the chin, or move it to y ≈ 1500. Shrink the top banner's font, or widen its box, so "LATER" fits (f01, f02), and drop the banner 0.5 s earlier or move it lower on phone shots so it never covers the clock (f02, 1461-1470). Scale the phone UI to about 92% so the bubbles, input field and send button stay inside the frame (f03, f19). Fix the "Trailer 1" overlap in f10, and fill the empty bottom of f04 (a road or skyline, or move the card down).
3. **World and light: put the inserts in the world and light the night flat.** Composite the calendar (f08), receipt (f13), poster (f11) and views screen (f10) into a set: on the fridge, coming out of a till on the counter, on a wall, on the TV in the flat. Use real perspective and shadows, as in REF 3_18 and REF 5_10. Raise the exposure of the night flat by about 1 stop on the characters and add a cool TV-coloured key and rim light, so Lucy's and Gordon's faces read (f05, f06, f09, f12, f18, f20). In the game shots f15, f16 and f17, keep the characters in focus (put the depth of field on the background), remove the post behind Gordon's head in f15, move the minimap or the character so they don't overlap, and add detail to the empty skies (clouds, skyline) in f15 and f17.
