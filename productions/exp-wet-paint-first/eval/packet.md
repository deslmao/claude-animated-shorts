## Earlier independent findings (phase 1, on the plot): for each, judge whether the viewer still meets the problem
- I1 (clarity): A first-time viewer can follow who and where, but three things blur. First, the deduction: tacky paint at 7:30 means it was painted between 6:45 and about 7:30, so seven o'clock is an arbitrary cutoff. Second, which day's sheet Mira is reading at 6:40. Third, which device took the cruel photo. The stake is also unclear: painting over a corner that holds someone else's graffiti doesn't obviously hurt Mira's mural. Each of these makes the viewer stop to work something out that the plot doesn't need.
- I2 (cause and effect): The story's ticking clock is broken twice, so the escalation goes slack. The viewer sees that the threat was empty, and E10 arrives as a scheduled event rather than a consequence. The fourth picture appears with no cause. Leo's line is a threat that nothing follows from.
- I3 (emotional investment): We care about Asha before the climax more than about Mira, and that's acceptable for a realisation story. But Mira's turn costs her nothing we can see. A confession painted on her own prize entry is potentially a big sacrifice, and the plot doesn't show it as one: no reaction from a judge or Okafor, no name signed, no risk to the prize stated. Deleting a two-week-old post is a cheap gesture by comparison.
- I4 (setup and payoff): E5 announces the reveal four events early. Once the viewer has seen the two orange straps side by side, E8 and E9 only confirm it. The payoff object (the orange-strap camera) and the evidence (a phone photo) don't match, so a careful viewer asks why Asha painted a camera. The fourth picture is a continuity break.
- I5 (plot hole): (1) **The obvious witness.** From 6:47 every morning a girl sits alone on the bench beside the wall, drawing (E1, and the rules). Mira passes her on Monday and never asks her what she saw until Thursday. The most-liked comment will be "She literally sits next to the wall every day, just ask her." (2) **The obvious stakeout.** After Monday, Mira could arrive at 6:45 instead of 7:30. She waits until Thursday (E6) without being shown a reason. (3) **The empty rule.** Okafor's "one more" is broken by the second and third pictures, and he doesn't act (E3, E5). Nobody at breakfast club notices a child painting the courtyard wall each morning beside the hall door either. (4) **The deduction.** The "before seven" cutoff doesn't follow from paint that's tacky at 7:30 (E4). (5) **The timeline.** Leo leaves "just before 7:00", but he's at the gate after Mira's 7:30 photographs (E2). (6) **The ending.** "Delete for everyone" on a two-week-old post isn't possible in common chat apps (E11), and it can't undo thirty-four views. Fixes: Mira asks Asha in E1 or E3, and Asha says "I didn't see anyone". That's a lie the sign-in sheet later exposes, which gives the viewer a checkable contradiction. Give Mira a reason to start early only on Thursday (the third picture shows the camera, so it's now personal). Re-time Okafor's warning and Leo's gate appearance. Replace the deletion with an apology made to the same chat.
- I6 (consistency): The packet contradicts itself in five places. (a) The "one more" rule (E2) against two further pictures with no consequence (E3, E5). (b) Leo "just before 7:00" (rules) against Leo at the gate after 7:30 (E2). (c) Thursday 6:40, before the 6:45 opening, against the 6:47 and 6:55 entries on the sheet (E6). (d) Three pictures in E10 against five in E11, with Mira painting only one. (e) The figure "lifts a camera on an orange strap" (E5) against the photo "on her phone" (E9). The table's other objects are consistent: the brush passes from Asha (E7) to Mira (E11), the roller stays unused, and the sketchbook stays with Asha.
- I7 (dilemma balance): For A: Leo's threat, the spray tags, his suspension, and his presence at the hall door at 6:55 (E2, E6). For B: a girl matching the painted girl sits by the wall (E1); the brush strokes, not spray (E3); the panels form a narrative (E3, E5); the green smudge by Asha's name (E6). B wins decisively at E1 and E3, and A gains no evidence afterwards. That's acceptable when the real question is "why" and "who is the laughing figure", but Leo isn't a working red herring. Either give A a piece of evidence that cuts both ways (Leo had green spray paint at the bike sheds; Leo's brother is in the laughing group) or treat Leo as background and spend the seconds on the realisation.
- I8 (emotional cost): Before the climax, the loss is "that corner gets painted over", which is shown, but the threat is never enforced (E3, E5) and isn't clearly a loss to Mira's own work. The deeper loss, her image of herself, lands at E9 and works. At the end, her redemptive act has no visible price: the prize risk isn't stated, and nobody reacts to the fifth picture. Show what she risks by keeping the corner (a judge reads the panels; her name is under them).
- I9 (cold viewer): Summary: "Someone keeps painting little comic panels on Mira's mural, she thinks it's the bad boy, but it's the lonely new girl, and the panels show Mira bullying her with a photo. Mira realises, stops the teacher from painting over it, adds a panel of herself sitting with the girl, and actually sits with her." The viewer would misunderstand or question several things. They'd guess the girl at E1 and Mira at E5, so the middle plays as confirmation. They'd ask why the panels show a camera when the photo is on a phone, and why the teacher ignored his own rule for two days. They'd ask where the fourth panel came from. They might read the fifth panel as Mira taking over Asha's work, unless it's clearly an apology (her name on it, Asha's reaction).
