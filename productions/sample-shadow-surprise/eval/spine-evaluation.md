# Independent evaluation: The First Surprise
packet: 8077a47b

## Criteria
### clarity
- result: weak
- beats: E1, E2, E3, E6
- evidence: The rule is stated as "your shadow acts out what you truly want, a moment before you do", but E1 shows a shadow pouring soup into a plant pot, which the man never goes on to do. That reads as "the shadow shows your real wish", not "a preview of your next move". E6's juggling hats are a wish, not a next move either. E2's failed surprise party shows guests' shadows jumping up from behind the sofa "before the lights go on". By rule 2 a dark room has no shadows, so the picture contradicts the rule the viewer has just learned. E1 to E3 are three separate world gags (café, poker sign, dark office, failed party, painter) before Milo appears in E4/E5.
- problem: The viewer gets two readings of the core rule, and the one picture meant to show why surprise parties fail breaks rule 2. That leaves them unsure why Milo's dark-room plan should work when the classic dark-room surprise apparently doesn't. With so much world before the protagonist, a casual viewer gets five worldbuilding images before learning whose story it is.
- fix: Simplify the rule to "your shadow acts out what you truly want" and drop "a moment before you do" (no event needs it). Redraw E2 so the failure follows the rules: the guests are in the dark, but the host's shadow mimes "SURPRISE!" all afternoon, or light from the opening door falls across the guests and their shadows leap. Compress E2 and E3 so Milo is on screen by about the third beat. The painter (E3) could be someone Milo watches, or Milo himself absorbed in something.

### cause and effect
- result: weak
- beats: E6, E7, E10, E11
- evidence: E6: Milo's shadow juggles party hats and points at the calendar, and "Gus raises an eyebrow". Milo pulls the blind, and the plot carries on as if the secret survived. E10: Milo, supposedly absorbed, spots a guest's shadow and kills the light "without looking up". E11: a domino tips a wall light switch.
- problem: The chain breaks at E6. Gus has had 79 fake-surprise birthdays, and he has just seen party hats and a calendar pointed at on his birthday. Nothing that follows undoes that, so the "real" surprise at E12 is unearned: the most surprise-proof man alive has had a clear tip-off. At E10 the rule should make Milo's shadow react the moment his attention leaves the dominoes, yet it doesn't. The success comes from the rules being bent, not followed. At E11, a falling domino flipping a wall switch is a physics claim viewers will dispute.
- fix: E6: Milo slams the blind before Gus looks up, so Gus sees only the blind. Or keep the eyebrow and add a beat where Milo deliberately misdirects (for example, he lets Gus "discover" a decoy so Gus thinks the party is next week), which also escalates the cleverness. E10: let Milo's shadow flicker toward the guest for half a second, so the cost of breaking focus is visible, and have him fix it with something he can reach in his eyeline (nudging the door shut with the next domino tray). E11: end the run on something a domino can plausibly trigger (a marble down a ramp onto a lamp's pull-chain or a push-button lamp switch), and show the mechanism in one shot.

### emotional investment
- result: weak
- beats: E4, E5, E12
- evidence: E4: a montage of fake gasps while Gus's shadow yawns. E5: Milo circles the date. E12: Gus's real gasp, his shadow leaping higher than anyone's, and Milo's shadow hugging Gus's shadow.
- problem: The yawning shadow says Gus is bored by parties, not that he secretly longs to be surprised. The stated loss ("proved Gus right that it can't be done") never appears on screen: Gus never claims it, and nothing shows that this matters to Milo. As planned, the stakes are "a nicer birthday", so the climax is sweet but low-pressure.
- fix: Use the rule, which is the story's best tool. In E4 (or a new beat near it), Gus's face says "nobody can surprise me, and that's fine", while his shadow shows what he truly wants: it looks wistfully at a child being genuinely surprised, or mimes a gasp to itself. Milo sees it, and that is his reason in E5. The E12 leap then pays off a wish we saw, not just a yawn.

### setup and payoff
- result: pass
- beats: E2, E3, E4, E7, E8, E12
- evidence: Darkness removes shadows (E2), which the plan uses for the guests (E7). Absorption makes the shadow copy you (E3, the painter), which the plan uses for Milo and Gus (E8) and which gets tested (E9). The yawning shadow at past parties (E4) pays off as the shadow leaping highest (E12). Milo's shadow hugging Gus's shadow before the real hug is a strong, rule-based final image.
- problem: (minor) Gus's love of dominoes ("Gus's eyes light up", E8) and the living-room switch as the run's target appear only when they're used. That is acceptable for a surprise mechanism, but planting either would make E8 feel less convenient.
- fix: (optional) Put a domino set or an old domino-run photo in the E4 montage.

### originality and adaptation
- result: pass
- beats: E1 to E12
- evidence: The declared sources are used as concepts only: a shadow with a mind of its own (Peter Pan), an honesty compulsion (Liar Liar), and a chain-reaction reveal. None of Peter Pan's or Liar Liar's events, characters or conflicts appear. The story has its own goal (a grandson's gift), obstacle, plan and payoff. "A world where your shadow shows your true feelings" is a familiar writing-prompt premise, but this execution (surprise parties as the casualty, absorption as the loophole, the shadow hug) is its own.
- problem: (note, not a fail) The shape (one rule, comic proofs, a planted exception, the protagonist uses the exception) is the generic world-rule template it shares with *1,000,000 Steps*. It doesn't reproduce that story's distinctive events (no countdown, no rescue, no ripple ending), but stories in this format should vary the shape (benchmarks §4.11).
- fix: none required.

## Stress tests
### plot hole
- applies: yes, there are several obstacles and a world-wide rule
- result: fail
- beats: E2, E4, E6, E7, E10, E11
- finding: (1) **Gus has seen the hats (E6).** His raised eyebrow means he suspects, and nothing restores the secret. This is the hole that breaks the ending. (2) **Why has the dark trick never worked before?** In E2 this world already holds job interviews in the dark, so hiding guests in the dark is the obvious fix everyone would know. Yet E4 shows 79 years of guests hiding in light. The viewer needs to see what specifically defeats ordinary dark-room surprises (the host's shadow beforehand, or light from the door), so Milo's plan visibly beats that exact failure. (3) **"Why didn't Milo just stay away from Gus all day?"** Between breakfast (E6) and 6 PM (E7/E8), "he lives with Gus" isn't enough. One visible reason (Gus asks Milo to stay home with him, or Milo's job is to keep Gus out of the living room) closes it. (4) Top-comment probes: "dominoes can't flip a light switch" (E11), and "if Milo was absorbed, how did he notice the guest?" (E10). Fixes are under cause and effect.

### consistency
- applies: yes, three stated rules and a timeline
- result: fail
- beats: E1, E2, E6, E9, E10
- finding: E2's shadows appear in an unlit room, which contradicts rule 2 ("In the dark, nobody has a shadow"), and this is the picture that explains the central obstacle. "A moment before you do" doesn't match E1 (the soup is never poured) or E6 (Milo never juggles). E10 has an absorbed Milo noticing and reacting to something off his task with no shadow reaction, which contradicts rule 3 as E9 has just shown it (one stray thought made his shadow dance). The rest is consistent: guests in the dark have no shadows, absorbed builders' shadows copy them, and the past-party yawn matches the final leap.

### dilemma balance
- applies: no, there's no viewer choice or competing explanations; it's a solution ending

### emotional cost
- applies: yes
- result: weak
- beats: E4, E5
- finding: If Milo fails, Gus gets another fake gasp. The only sign Gus minds is a yawning shadow, which reads as boredom, not quiet disappointment. Milo's investment is a calendar circle. The stated cost ("proving Gus right") is never shown. Show Gus's shadow wishing to be surprised (it's the rule's natural use) and let Milo see it, so both the want and the loss are on screen before E8.

### reproduction
- applies: yes, three inspirations are declared and the world-rule format has a benchmark
- result: pass
- beats: E1 to E12
- finding: **Peter Pan**: the concept of an independent shadow only. No shadow-chasing, no sewing, no Neverland. **Liar Liar**: an honesty compulsion only. Its conflict (a lawyer's career and his son) and solution aren't used. **1,000,000 Steps** (the closest benchmark, by format): compared on the five points. Sequence: both have a rule, comic proofs and a planted exception, but no countdown, emergency, frozen bystanders or ripple ending here. Motivation: a gift for a grandparent vs. saving a baby. Conflict: keeping a secret vs. a limited resource. Solution: deliberate absorption plus a domino trigger vs. a handstand loophole. Payoff meaning: a first real feeling vs. a town copying the hero. These are shared tropes, not reproduced expression.

### cold viewer
- applies: yes
- result: weak
- beats: E1 to E12
- finding: A viewer would think: "In a world where shadows give away what you really want, nobody can be surprised. A kid keeps his own shadow quiet by building a huge domino run with his grandpa, so the grandpa gets his first real surprise party at 80." They would likely misunderstand or question four things. Why the E2 party guests had shadows in the dark. Why Gus, who saw the party hats at breakfast, is still surprised. Whether the shadow shows wishes or upcoming actions. How a domino flipped a wall switch. A viewer who misses the painter beat (E3) won't get why the domino run is the plan at all, until E9 demonstrates it.

## Overall
- keep-watching: yes. The soup-into-the-plant opener is a strong, instantly readable hook, and the shadow gags are fun. The risk of leaving is at E2/E3, a fourth and fifth world gag before the protagonist appears. Attentive viewers will also feel cheated at E12 if Gus visibly saw the giveaway at E6.
- verdict: revise
- top-fixes: 1. Close the E6 hole. Either Gus doesn't see the shadow (the blind drops before he looks up), or Milo actively misdirects him afterwards, so the E12 surprise is earned. 2. Make the rules airtight. Drop "a moment before you do". Redraw E2 so ordinary surprises fail for a reason that obeys rule 2 (the host's shadow leaks it, or light from the door hits the guests), and make Milo's plan beat exactly that failure. At E10, show Milo's shadow flicker when he breaks focus, and have him fix it with something in reach. Replace the domino-flips-wall-switch with a trigger a domino can plausibly work. 3. Put the emotional cost on screen. Early on, Gus's shadow shows he secretly wishes he could be surprised, and Milo sees it. That gives Milo his reason and turns the E12 leap into the payoff of a wish. Also tighten E1 to E3 so Milo appears by about the third beat.
