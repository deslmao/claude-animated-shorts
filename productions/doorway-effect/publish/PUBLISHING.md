**Description:**
```
You walk into a room and instantly forget why. Scientists think the doorway might be part of the reason.

In experiments by psychologist Gabriel Radvansky, people who carried objects through a doorway forgot more about them than people who walked the same distance within one room. One explanation: your brain seems to split experience into "events", and a doorway acts as an event boundary, so what you planned in the old room gets harder to reach. It's a simplified picture: the memory isn't deleted.

The effect isn't universal. Later studies found it mostly when people's minds were busy. And going back to the first room can sometimes help, because it's full of cues, but it's no guarantee.

References:
• Radvansky, Krawietz & Tamplin (2011). Walking through doorways causes forgetting: Further explorations. Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology.
• McFadyen et al. (2021). Doorways do not always cause forgetting: a multimodal investigation. BMC Psychology.
```
