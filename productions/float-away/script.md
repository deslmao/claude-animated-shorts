# Thinkora Video 08: "In This World, If Nobody Thinks About You, You Float Away". Script v3 (final for approval)

**Format:** story-first emotional short with comedy beats, about 88–89 s, Milo narrating in the first person,
with in-scene dialogue.

**Status:** v3, the final focused pass (Juno's reason to share, room for the reveal, a consistent world rule);
awaiting approval. Nothing has been generated or rendered.

**History:** v1 told about 16 developments, mostly through narration. v2 rewrote it to the new story-density
standard and showed 38 on-screen developments. v3 is a focused pass on v2: the same premise, twist and ending.

---

## Stage A: Concept (unchanged)

**Premise:** In this world, the more people think about you, the heavier you get. When Milo's grandpa Gus starts
floating away, Milo has to make the whole world think about him.

**Characters:**
- **Milo:** a good kid who drifted away without noticing.
- **Gus:** the funniest, heaviest grandpa on the street. He cheats at everything, burns everything, and turns
  every problem into a joke, which is exactly how he hides his own.
- **Juno:** the classmate with two million followers who has never once been nice to Milo. Bored, eye-rolling,
  secretly decent.
- **The rival politician:** a heckler who steals the crowd's attention.
- **The movie star and his weary assistant.**

**The twist:** "He started floating the spring I stopped visiting. I was the last one."

**The ending:** a call to action: "He might be holding onto the counter."

- **Two lines cut:** "He dropped a little, then kept rising." and "We have pancake wars again." are now shown, not said.
- **One line added:** Juno's.

- **If the narration comes in slow,** the first cut is the 1.2 s checkers insert (shot 9).

### The three fixes
1. **Juno's share is earned.**
   - **Planted:** in shot 6, Gus hands a grinning Juno her repaired bike while kids queue.
   - **Paid off:** in shots 31–32 she sees the stream, rolls her eyes, and is about to swipe away. Then she
     recognises Gus. A 1-second flashback to the bike, "Ugh. He fixed my bike.", and she shares it with the caption
     "this man fixed my bike when I was 7. milo is still annoying".
   - **Why it works:** it is her own reason (gratitude to Gus, not kindness to Milo), it stays in character, and it
     has a joke.
2. **The reveal has room to land.**
   - "I was the last one" is followed by about 5 s with no narration.
   - **Shot 26:** silence, then a slow pull back from Milo sitting on the kerb in the rain, with Gus a speck among
     the stars.
   - **Under that pull back:** one soft music-box note of the pancake-war tune.
   - **Shot 27:** Milo wipes his face, looks at the phone in his hand and stands up. His decision is shown, not
     narrated.
   - **Then the livestream:** it starts from a low, slow build.
3. **The world rule is unmistakable.** One visual and sound language is used everywhere:
   - **Attention lands:** heads turn toward the person, they sink or crush what's under them, and a heavy thunk
     plays.
   - **Attention leaves:** heads turn away, the person's feet lift, and a soft rising whoosh plays.
   - **The politician now shows both directions in one gag.** The crowd chants his name and he sinks to his knees.
     The rival shouts "Free pizza over here!", every head turns away, and he pops out of the cobbles and floats,
     clutching the podium. (v2's heckle made the crowd laugh *at* him, which is still attention, so it was
     inconsistent.)
   - **The same language runs through the whole story:**
     - the bench man: nobody looks, so he floats;
     - Gus's street: people walk past without looking, so his heels lift;
     - the video call: while Milo looks, Gus sinks a little; when Milo hangs up, Gus drifts back up alone. This is
       a fair clue to the twist.
     - the shouted memories: Gus dips;
     - the windows lighting up: Gus drops.
