# The Extra Door, Part 2 (rewrite): three story directions

**Status:** for approval. The old Part 2 is set aside. No narration, assets, storyboard or render.

## Fixed continuity from Part 1
- Milo's hallway has seven doors; tonight there were eight.
- The extra door is old, dark and has no handle. An old nail sits at eye level where the photo of Milo and Juno used to
  hang, and the photo was found on the floor, leaning against the door.
- The hallway clock stopped, then three knocks came.
- Juno's voice came from behind the door, repeating the exact same take.
- The real Juno texted: "No, I'm still at work. Why?"
- The voice answered: "Don't text her."
- A brass handle pushed out through the solid wood and turned. The audience voted **B: RUN DOWNSTAIRS**.

---

## Direction 1: "The Fourteenth Step" (a loop inside the house)
**Premise:** the stairs won't let Milo leave. Every time he runs down, he arrives back at the top, and the hallway
has gained another door.

**Major events:**
1. He bolts down the stairs (the vote callback).
2. He counts thirteen steps, then a fourteenth he's never felt, and lands at the top again.
3. Nine doors now.
4. He tries the hallway window: it looks out onto the same hallway.
5. He calls the real Juno. She keeps him counting while she drives home.
6. Each run adds a door, and one of the new doors is his own bedroom door, from the inside.
7. He remembers the nail and hangs the photo back on it. The extra doors fold away one by one.

**Twist:** the photo was what had kept the door out. Someone took it down.

**Connects to Part 1:** the nail, the photo, the stopped clock, the counting.

**Why watch to the end:** the "loop" puzzle, plus the reveal of who moved the photo. In the final shot Juno's car
pulls up, and the knock comes from the front door.

**Weakness:** it stays in one location, and it repeats the counting idea, which is exactly what Part 1 was criticised
for.

---

## Direction 2: "Juno's Night Shift" (escape, a journey, the lab reveal)
**Premise:** Milo escapes the house and runs through the night to the real Juno at her lab. There he learns that the
door was never after him: it is something Juno has been studying, and it travels through photographs.

**Major events (about 16 developments):**
1. **The vote callback:** B lights up, and Milo vaults down the stairs.
2. **The front door won't open.** The keys aren't on their hook. He climbs out of the kitchen window.
3. **The night street in the rain:** as he runs, every shop door he passes knocks three times, one after another,
   following him down the street.
4. **A text from "Juno":** "Come back home." Seconds later the real Juno calls: "Don't go near any door. Come to the
   lab."
5. **Juno's lab** (a new setting), late at night. Juno, real and in her lab coat, pulls him inside. A reunion with
   real warmth.
6. **He shows her the photo he grabbed.** She goes pale and turns her monitor round: night-vision footage of an old
   door standing alone in a sealed test room, labelled "SPECIMEN 8".
7. **Her explanation, in one line:** it copies the voice of whoever you trust most, and it can only appear where a
   photo of you hangs.
8. **Escalation:** on the lab wall, beside her desk, hangs the very same photo of Milo and Juno. The wall beneath it
   darkens into wood grain.
9. **Juno's voice comes from the new door** ("Milo, it's me, let me in"), while Juno is standing right next to him.
10. **Then Milo's own voice comes from the door:** "Juno, it's me. Open up." It has learned both of them.
11. **The brass handle pushes through the wood** (the Part 1 image) and starts to turn.
12. **The plan fails:** Juno grabs the photo to tear it, and the frame won't come off the wall. It's grown into it.
13. **Milo's decision:** he remembers the nail in his hallway. The door doesn't need the photo; it needs them both
    to be looking at it. He tells Juno: "Don't look at it. Look at me."
14. **The climax:** they turn their backs to it, eyes locked on each other. The voices plead, then scream. Silence.
15. **The release:** the handle sinks back into the wall, the grain fades, and the wall is plain plaster.
16. **The ending:** Juno exhales. Milo laughs with relief. Juno's phone buzzes. A text from Milo's number, sent from
    Milo's empty house: "I'm home. Come let me in." A hard cut.

**Twist:**
- The door belongs to Juno's work, and it hunts through the photo of the two of them.
- The way to beat it isn't to destroy it but to refuse to look at it: you trust each other instead of the voice.
- The sting at the end: it has moved into Milo's identity.

**Connects to Part 1:**
- "I'm still at work" pays off at the lab.
- The photo and the nail are the rule.
- The exact-repeat voice and the handle that grows through the wood both return.
- It follows the audience's choice to run.

**Why watch to the end:**
- The episode keeps moving: house, street, lab.
- Every scene adds a rule or a threat.
- The reveal reframes Part 1 (the photo was the trap all along).
- The climax is a clear, visual choice.
- The final text makes people comment.

**It's a complete film:** escape, journey, ally, reveal, climax, resolution, and a short sting. It isn't a teaser.

## Direction 3: "The Milo Who Came Back" (an identity twist)
**Premise:** Milo runs downstairs and finds Juno home early in the kitchen. They barricade the hallway together, but
the story slowly reveals that the Milo we have been following came *out* of the door.

**Major events:**
1. The vote callback.
2. Juno in the kitchen, keys in hand.
3. They wedge a chair under the stairs door.
4. Small wrong details: Milo writes with the wrong hand, and his reflection in the oven door lags.
5. Knocking from the upstairs door, now in Milo's voice: "Juno, it's me, don't let him near you."
6. Juno has to decide which Milo is real.
7. She tests him with a memory only the real one would know, and he gets it right.
8. The real Milo stops knocking.

**Twist:** our narrator-protagonist is the copy. The "right" answer came from listening to them for weeks.

**Connects to Part 1:** "Don't text her" was the real Milo trying to warn Juno, not a threat.

**Why watch to the end:** a dramatic reversal and a rewatch twist.

**Weakness:** the twist depends on subtle clues that are hard to read at 9:16 Short speed. It also makes the hero
the villain, which could confuse a young audience and leaves no satisfying ending.

---

## Recommendation: Direction 2, "Juno's Night Shift"
- **Density and variety:** three escalating settings (house, a rainy street of knocking doors, the lab) and about 16
  distinct events. Nothing relies on repeated counting or pauses, which fixes Part 1's main weakness.
- **It completes the mystery:** it explains the door, the voice and the photo with one simple, visual rule. It
  rewards Part 1 viewers: the nail, "still at work", the handle.
- **Character interaction:** Milo and Juno are finally together on screen, and the climax is a choice made between
  them ("Look at me"), not a jump scare.
- **A real ending plus a sting:** the threat is beaten, and the final text ("I'm home. Come let me in.") gives a
  comment hook without being a cliffhanger tease.
- **Production fit:** it reuses the hallway and downstairs sets, the city night street, the lab set, the phone
  screen, the photo frame, the old door and the knock sounds. New pieces are small: a "Specimen 8" monitor screen, a
  wall-door effect (the grain growing), and shop-door knocks.

**Estimated length:** about 160–190 spoken words, about **75–85 s**.
