## Experiment dimensions
- clarity: 4, who, where and stakes are always clear, but the key sheet clue relies on a name ("Asha K.") the viewer has never heard, and beat 6 is dense.
- curiosity: 4, "Still wet" and a new panel each morning pull the viewer forward, though "who" is guessable by beat 2, so the pull shifts to "why".
- viewer participation: 4, the viewer can catch Asha's lie on the sheet, rule Leo out by the brush strokes, and read the comic panels ahead of Mira.
- cause and effect: 4, every event now follows from the one before (warning, stakeout, sheet, confrontation, walk-away, fourth panel, roller), with only Leo's threat left as a loose thread.
- emotional investment: 4, "Nobody sees me" and "Thirty-four people laughed. So did she." land hard, while Mira's price is real but slightly blurred by the judge's ambiguous look.
- visual communication: 4, the story survives the mute test almost fully, but many decisive clues are miniatures that need careful full-frame inserts.
- reveal effectiveness: 4, the orange strap turns Mira's opening ritual into the weapon, and the fourth and fifth panels mirror real actions; an attentive viewer suspects it at beat 5.
- immersion: 3, a narrated third-person account with clean craft, but it lacks the sensory, second-person pull of the immersive benchmarks.
- pacing: 4, a new fact every beat, a well-placed silent beat 10, and only beat 6 sags with exposition.
- originality: 4, a victim's comic strip painted onto the bully's prize entry, finished by the bully herself, is a fresh engine with no benchmark skeleton.
- strengths: the comic-strip panels are both the evidence and the storytelling, with the fourth panel predicting the walk-away and the fifth acted out on the real bench; the investigator turns out to be the culprit through a fairly planted object (the orange strap) and double-edged lines ("I didn't see anyone" / "Nobody sees me"); and it reads strongly with the sound off.
- weaknesses: the "who" is solved by beat 2–4, with Leo cleared almost at once, so the middle runs on "why" alone; the girl is never named before the sheet that exposes her, and beat 6's deduction is dense; and the judge's reaction leaves it unclear whether the confession costs Mira anything or wins her the prize.
