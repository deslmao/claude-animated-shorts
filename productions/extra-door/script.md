# Video 05: The Extra Door (Part 1), script v1 for approval

Interactive horror, episode 1. About 53 s. Status: **waiting for the owner's approval**. No narration, assets,
storyboard or render yet.

**Voices:** narrator (calm and low, used only twice), Milo (whispering), Juno (heard only through the door).
**Setting:** Milo's upstairs hallway at night. Seven cream doors with brass handles. The top of the stairs and a
banister at the near end (the escape route for choice B). One old, dark, narrow wooden door at the far end, centred,
with no handle and no keyhole.

## Why this hook
- **It starts mid-action, with no setup.** The first frame already asks "eight what?", and "eight?" lands at about
  1.5 s.
- **The narrator contradicts the picture.** We see eight doors while the narrator calmly says seven, and "It always
  has" tells us he has lived here for years without explaining anything.
- **The viewer counts too,** which sets up the participation at the end from the very first shot.

## The fear curve
Each beat adds something new: a wrong number, a picture that was moved, the clock stopping, knocks, shapes under the
door, a familiar voice, the same words repeated exactly, proof that it isn't Juno, and then proof that it can see him.
There is no jump scare. The loudest sound in the film is a small latch click.

## Why this ending
- The handle that grows through solid wood resolves "no handle on his side" and is unmistakable on screen.
- "Don't text her" turns the mystery from "who is it?" into "what is it, and how does it know?"
- Milo asks the viewer directly, which makes the vote feel like it matters.
- Both choices are dangerous and open-ended. Part 2 can follow either:
  - **A:** what is behind the door;
  - **B:** he runs downstairs, and the staircase has one more step than it should.

## Clues for rewatches
- The nail in the door is where the picture used to hang.
- The clock stops before the knocks.
- The voice is an exact repeat.
