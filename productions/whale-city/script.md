# The Whale City: world mechanics and script (approved; plain-language pass applied)

Format: high-concept world story, narrated in the third person with in-scene dialogue. About 105 s.
Production constraint: the current pipeline, cameras, library and sound as they are. New set and prop assets only
where the story needs them.

## World mechanics (one focused pass)
**Why the city is on a whale.**
- Three hundred years ago, the founders' ship found the only land for weeks of sailing: a long, warm, sheltered island with a spring at its peak. They built a city on it.
- It was a sleeping whale. By the time they found out, the city was built and there was nowhere else to go.
- The "spring" at the top of the city is its blowhole. The city built its square around it and called it the Fountain.

**Why everyone must be quiet.**
- At their first harvest festival, the founders fired a cannon. The island woke: it rolled, breathed out so hard it blew the roofs off, and then went back to sleep.
- The founders concluded that noise wakes it, and next time it might dive and take the city down with it.
- So the rule is: never make a sound. Felt slippers, hand signs, whispers.
- The founders' ship's bell hangs in the square with its clapper tied: the one thing loud enough to wake it.

**Why its breathing is weakening.**
- A sleeping whale takes slow, shallow breaths.
- To honour "the breath", every new mayor adds a carved marble ring around the Fountain. There are twelve, one every twenty-five years or so.
- Each ring makes the hole a little narrower. Each shallow breath takes in a little less air.
- So the whale is slowly suffocating, and as it weakens it sits lower in the water.

**Why nobody has realised.**
- The change in one lifetime is tiny: one ring and a hand's width of spout.
- The rings are a sacred tradition, so nobody questions them.
- The weaker spout is blamed on age ("it's an old whale"), and the flooded harbour steps on the tides.
- Only one record spans the generations: the breath-keeper's pole, with every keeper's dawn marks of the spout's height.
- Wren, the new breath-keeper, is the first to set those marks against the rings.

**What happens when it wakes.**
- The founders' story is also the evidence: awake, it breathes hard enough to blow roofs off.
- A whale that can't breathe doesn't dive; it fights for the surface.
- So waking it is the only way to give it a breath strong enough to clear its own airway.
- Its lungs fill, it floats higher, and the city rises out of the water it was sinking into.
- Then it stretches, swims a short way, and sleeps again, breathing freely.
- The people are already crowded on the high square, fleeing the flood, holding the railings. Roof tiles rattle, but nobody is lost.

**How the ending is spectacular and still believable.** Every big image was set up earlier:
- The founders' blown-off roofs lead to the marble rings blasting into the sky.
- The sinking city leads to the city rising and streaming with water.
- The tied bell leads to the first loud sound in the film.
- The low marks on the pole lead to Wren's last mark at the very top.

Its eye and its tail appear only once it's awake.

**Sound concept:** the whole film is whispers, felt footsteps, sea and the whale's slow breath, until the bell. The bell, the wake-up and the city's first cheer are the loudest moments.

## Assets this story needs (current system)
**Reused:**
- The `oldtown` square (cobbles, market stalls, houses) as the city's streets and square.
- The sea and water surface from the island and ship sets.
- Day, golden and night light.
- The cast generator: Wren, Gran, the Mayor and townsfolk.
- The camera library: closeUp, overShoulder, insert, pushIn, follow and the aerial reveal.
- Motion blur and depth of field.
- `amb_sea`, `amb_square`, the mystery music.

**New production assets, one theme:**
- The whale-city set: the whale's back in open sea, carrying the city, with harbour walls at the waterline.
- The Fountain prop: the blowhole with 12 and 13 marble rings, a spout rig (low, failing, erupting with the rings flying, tall), and the breath-keeper's pole with the marks insert.
- The founders' tapestry insert and the founders' bell (clapper tied, then cut).
- The whale's eye (shut, opening) and its tail (rising, falling).
- A flood plane for the harbour streets.
- SFX: a whale groan, a spout whoosh, the ship's bell, a crowd cheer, felt footsteps.

**Watch items, each with an in-story fallback:**
- **The city gliding forward:** shown by the sea moving past and the wake, not by moving the set.
- **The erupting spout:** a rig in the style of the One Too Many smoke plume.
