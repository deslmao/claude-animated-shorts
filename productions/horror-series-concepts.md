# Horror mini-series: five concepts (for the owner's choice)

2026-11-15. Each series is 4 or 5 Shorts, with one continuous mystery, a revelation in every episode, a cliffhanger
that changes the direction of the story, an audience A/B vote before the finale, and a real payoff. No scripts or
production yet.

**Built from our lessons:**
- **One visible rule per series**, like One Too Many's count. Every series has an on-screen object that carries the mystery: a camera feed, a water line, a film counter.
- **A protagonist we like in the first 10 seconds.**
- **Someone they love is at stake.**
- **The vote must cost the viewer something either way,** and each branch gets its own produced finale.

**How the vote works:**
- The penultimate episode ends on the A/B question, with a pinned "comment A or B" prompt.
- About 48 hours later, we produce only the finale for the winning branch.
- Both branches are outlined in advance, so the vote is real.

## 1. FORGET TOBY (5 episodes)
**Logline:** Lily's doorbell camera shows her sleepwalking into the garden every night at 3:03 to bury things belonging to a little brother she doesn't have.

**Protagonist:** Lily, 14: funny, stubborn, notices everything.

**Central mystery:** who is Toby, and why is Lily erasing him?

**Rule (revealed in episode 3):** *it finds children through the people who remember them.* Forgotten children are invisible to it.

**Visible device:** the doorbell-camera feed, with its timestamp. Every episode has a 3:03 clip.

| Ep | Title | The revelation | Cliffhanger (the direction change) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3:03 a.m. | The figure on the doorbell camera burying a box is Lily, asleep. She digs it up: drawings by "Toby, age 7", one of them of her holding a boy's hand, captioned "Lily, don't forget me." | On the back, in Lily's own handwriting: **"DON'T DIG. — Lily."** She warned herself. |
| 2 | The Airing Cupboard | The "airing cupboard" is a boy's bedroom: dinosaur wallpaper, and height marks on the door frame next to hers, labelled "T". Mum and Dad look straight at it and see towels. | The 3:03 feed now shows a tall figure in a long coat at the end of the street. The next night, it's two houses closer. **There's a hunter.** |
| 3 | Voice Note | A year-old voice note in Lily's own crying voice: "It finds him through us. Every time we remember him, it gets closer. Mum, Dad and I forgot him on purpose. It was the only way." Toby is alive and hiding. | The figure is at the gate. From inside the sealed room, a small voice: **"Lily? You remembered me."** She has led it straight to him. |
| 4 | Remember or Forget | Lily plays the voice note to Mum and Dad, and they remember Toby too, which makes it worse. The figure knocks at the door in Toby's own knock. The family has until 3:03. | **VOTE:** **A: forget him again.** He's safe, but Lily will never know she had a brother. **B: remember him and fight for him.** He comes home, but it knows exactly where he is. |
| 5 | Finale | **A:** The family records new voice notes, buries the drawings and falls asleep holding hands. In the morning, a happy family of three. Final 3:03 clip: a small boy in the garden leaves a new drawing on the doorstep: "Thank you, Lily." **B:** The family says his name together and puts his photos back on the walls. Toby walks out of the cupboard and into Mum's arms, and the figure on the feed turns away. Final 3:03 clip: **Mum sleepwalking into the garden, carrying a box of Lily's drawings.** Now it's hunting Lily. | (B leaves a hook for a second series.) |

**Why it works:**
- The opening is a visible impossibility: you see yourself on camera.
- Each episode answers the last episode's question and asks a bigger one: who am I burying, then where is he, then why I hid him, then what's coming, then the choice.
- The vote is love against safety, and neither answer is free.
- It fits our existing pipeline: the home, hallway and night-street sets, the CCTV overlay look, and the sleepwalking walks.

## 2. LOW WATER (4 episodes)
**Logline:** a drought drains the reservoir over the village drowned in 1962, and every night the water drops, the village and its people come back, calling for the one child who left: Meg's grandmother.

**Protagonist:** Meg, 13, spending the summer with Gran Nell, who has started to forget things and is terrified of the water going down.

**Central mystery:** what happened the night Aldwick was flooded, and what does it want with Nell?

**Rule:** the lower the water, the more of the village returns.

**Visible device:** the reservoir's depth gauge, falling every episode.

| Ep | Title | The revelation | Cliffhanger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Steeple | The church steeple breaks the surface. At night its bell rings underwater, and there are lights in the drowned windows. | Fresh, wet child-sized footprints lead from the shoreline to Gran's back door. |
| 2 | The Street | The main street is exposed. Inside, the houses are dry, the tables are laid and every clock has stopped at 4:10. On a school desk is carved "NELL, YOU PROMISED TO COME BACK." | At dawn, Gran is standing waist-deep in the water, saying in a child's voice, "I'm coming." |
| 3 | What Nell Took | The villagers refused to leave. On flood night they rang the bell to gather everyone in the church. Ten-year-old Nell stole the bell's clapper so it couldn't call her, and ran. Without the bell, they can't rest. | Under Gran's bed is the clapper, and Gran is awake in the dark: "If you give it back, they'll take me with it." **VOTE:** **A: return the clapper.** The village rests, and Gran goes with them. **B: keep it and pray for rain.** Gran stays, and the village keeps waiting. |
| 4 | Finale | **A:** Meg rows Gran out to the steeple, and Gran rings the bell herself. The lights go out house by house, and on the church steps Nell is ten again, running to her mother. Meg rows back alone as the rain starts. **B:** The rain comes, the valley fills and Gran gets better. Years later, in another drought, the steeple rises again, and the bell rings Meg's name. | |

**Why it works:**
- The clock is a falling water line you can see.
- Grandmother and granddaughter is a bond everyone feels.
- The vote is a mercy question: let her go, or keep her and pass the debt on.

**Production cost:** a new reservoir and drowned-village set, and a water level that changes between episodes.

## 3. TOMORROW'S POLAROID (4 episodes)
**Logline:** Jess buys an old Polaroid camera that photographs the room exactly 24 hours later, and the man standing in tomorrow's photos gets closer every time.

**Protagonist:** Jess, 14, a camera-obsessed only child. The seller at the car-boot sale warned her: "Never photograph people."

**Central mystery:** who is the man, and what happened to the girl who owned the camera in 1987?

**Rule:** each photo shows that spot tomorrow. A pack holds 10 shots, and **when the counter hits 0, he takes the photographer.**

**Visible device:** the film counter (10 down to 0) and a photo slowly developing, which builds suspense every episode.

| Ep | Title | The revelation | Cliffhanger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shot 1 | A photo of her bedroom develops with a tall man standing in the corner. The next day the corner is empty, and a new photo shows him closer. The camera shows tomorrow. | A photo of tomorrow's family dinner: **Jess's chair is empty, and her parents are smiling.** |
| 2 | Clare | Jess finds the seller. The camera belonged to her daughter Clare, 14, who vanished in 1987. Clare's last photo, kept in a tin, shows the man holding Clare's hand. | Today's photo shows Clare, still 14 and in 1987 clothes, beside the man, mouthing **"RUN."** |
| 3 | Two Left | The rule: the man lives in tomorrow and takes whoever uses up the pack. Clare has been trapped in her last photo for 39 years. | Jess's little cousin grabs the camera and photographs Jess. **One shot left.** **VOTE:** **A: burn the camera.** He's gone forever, and so is Clare. **B: use the last shot on him.** This is the only way to free Clare, but if it fails, Jess goes in. |
| 4 | Finale | **A:** The camera melts, and in Clare's old photo she smiles and fades. Her mother lays a flower on the tin. **B:** Jess photographs the corner. The photo develops: the man is trapped in it, and Clare steps out into 2026, still 14, to her 80-year-old mother. Last frame: the photo is still developing, and behind the man, a second figure is coming into focus. It's Jess. | |

**Why it works:** the clearest rule of the five, with a countdown you can read like One Too Many's count, and a slowly developing photo to end every episode on.

**Risk:** the "cursed object" is a familiar horror device, so the Clare story has to carry the emotion.

## 4. ORDER FOR No. 13 (4 episodes)
**Logline:** a delivery rider keeps getting orders from 13 Hollin Lane, a house that burned down in 1996, and the girl inside is ordering from the night of the fire.

**Protagonist:** Ade, 17, who rides deliveries at night to pay towards his mum's rent.

**Central mystery:** who is ordering, and who started the fire?

**Rule:** the house is there only at 11:11 p.m.

**Visible device:** the delivery app, showing the order, the map gap between Nos. 11 and 15, and the customer's notes.

| Ep | Title | The revelation | Cliffhanger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Gap | At 11:11 a house stands in the gap. A small hand takes the bag through the letterbox, and the tip is in old pound notes. | The order note: "Same time tomorrow? Please bring batteries. The torch is dying and he's on the stairs." |
| 2 | Maisie | No. 13 burned in 1996, and Maisie Cole, 10, was never found. She's ordering from inside that night, from the cupboard under the stairs. | Through the letterbox, Ade sees the man on the stairs: **it's Mr Pike from No. 11, his kind regular customer, thirty years younger.** |
| 3 | The Neighbour | Pike started the fire. Now Pike knows what Ade has seen, and Ade's next order comes from No. 11: "Stop delivering." | Maisie's last order is a key to the front door. **VOTE:** **A: go in at 11:11 and pull her out.** She might live, but Ade might be trapped in 1996. **B: give her notes to the police.** Pike is caught, but Maisie stays in that night forever. |
| 4 | Finale | **A:** Smoke, the cupboard, they run out together. The gap is now a house, and a woman of 40 opens the door: "I've waited thirty years to tip you properly." **B:** Pike is arrested. At 11:11 the house appears one last time, with a final order: "No more deliveries. Thank you, Ade." It fades. | |

**Why it works:** a grounded, likeable working-class hero, a modern hook (the delivery app), and a human villain.

**Risk:** time-crossing rescues are a familiar idea, and it echoes The Other Jamie's buried-crime angle.

## 5. THE SNOW GLOBE (5 episodes)
**Logline:** Rosa's father vanished in a freak blizzard last Christmas. In his things she finds a snow globe holding a perfect model of their town, and a tiny figure in her own bedroom window waving up at her.

**Protagonist:** Rosa, 12, still setting a place for Dad.

**Central mystery:** where is Dad, and who made the globe?

**Rule:** shake the globe and it snows outside, for real.

**Visible device:** the globe, seen through a magnifying glass.

| Ep | Title | The revelation | Cliffhanger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | July Snow | She shakes the globe and it snows in July. Inside, a tiny light is on in her own window. | Through the magnifier, the tiny figure is **Dad**, waving. |
| 2 | The Toymaker | The globe was made in 1890. Dozens of townspeople have vanished in "blizzards" since then, and all of them are inside. | In the tiny park, Dad writes in the snow: "DON'T SHAKE IT. IT'S WATCHING YOU." |
| 3 | The Watcher | Something lives in the globe, and every shake lets it look out. It has chosen Rosa. | Rosa looks up at the real sky and sees a seam: a curved glass reflection. **Her town is inside a globe too.** |
| 4 | The Shelf | The town sits on a giant's shelf, and the blizzards are the giant shaking the globe. Dad fell into the small globe trying to get out. **VOTE:** **A: break the small globe.** Dad is freed, but so is the thing. **B: break the sky.** Everyone escapes into the unknown. | |
| 5 | Finale | The winning break is produced. | |

**Why it works:** the biggest "wait, what?" twist, and very visual.

**Risks:**
- It has two nested rules, against our one-rule lesson.
- It's harder to follow at Shorts speed, and the finale is the least resolvable.

## Strongest candidate: FORGET TOBY
- **It fixes The Other Jamie's weakness directly.** The person at stake is a seven-year-old brother. Lily's love for him comes before she remembers him, through her drawings, his height marks and his voice.
- **Every cliffhanger turns the story,** not just the scares:
  1. she's the intruder;
  2. he's real, and he's here;
  3. she hid him on purpose;
  4. she has led it to him;
  5. the choice.
- **Viewers can follow every episode on its own** through one device: the 3:03 doorbell clip, which also opens and closes every episode.
- **The vote is the theme.** A means safety without love; B means love with danger. B's ending, with Mum carrying Lily's drawings, sets up a second series if the first one performs.
- **It's the cheapest to make well.** We already have the home, hallway and street sets, the night lighting, the CCTV overlay and the sleepwalking walks. The new pieces are the boy's bedroom, the tall figure and the drawings.

**Runner-up: Tomorrow's Polaroid.** It has the clearest rule and countdown, and would make a good second series.
