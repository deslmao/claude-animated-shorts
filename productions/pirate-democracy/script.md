# Pirates ran surprisingly democratic ships
mood: adventure

- Some of history's most feared pirates ran their ships like tiny democracies.
- Picture a pirate crew in the early 1700s. Before anyone touches a sword, they sign a contract.
- It says how the loot is shared, and the captain gets only a little more than everyone else.
- Lose an arm in battle? The crew pays you from a shared fund.
- And the captain? He's elected.
- captain: I'd like to remind everyone that I'm very brave.
- If he's a coward or a bully, the crew can vote him out and pick someone new.
- A second elected officer, the quartermaster, hands out the food and loot, so the captain can't grab it all.
- Wait, pirates were fair?
- You see, merchant ships had owners on land, and their captains could beat sailors into line.
- Pirates had no owners. The crew was the company, so they wrote rules to stop anyone becoming a tyrant.
- Economist Peter Leeson argues it was the only way a ship of outlaws could work.
- It wasn't perfect, and the rules varied from ship to ship.
- But some crews paid for injuries long before most countries had anything like it.
- So the scariest thing on the high seas might have been a pirate committee meeting.
