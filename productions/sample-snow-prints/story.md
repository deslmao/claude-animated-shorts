# One Set of Footprints
spec: 2
format: immersive+mystery
why: The viewer wakes into an impossible clue (footprints that come in and never leave) and investigates alongside the protagonist; two explanations (an intruder inside vs something else) gain visible evidence; the decisive clue (your missing boots) lets the viewer solve it a beat early; and the reveal recontextualises every earlier image, ending with a warm payoff.
length: 80
voice: georgeHushed
voices: you=liam, ivy=jessica
mood: tense
factual: no

## Premise
- question: Footprints come out of the woods to your door and none go back. Who came in, and where are they now?
- protagonist: you (the viewer, as the older sibling), home alone at night with your little sister Ivy
- want: to keep Ivy safe while Mum is at work
- fear: that someone is inside the house with you both
- protects: Ivy
- decision: to stop searching the house and follow the tracks into the woods before the snow fills them
- loses: Ivy, if you read the clues wrong; the snow is starting again and will erase the only trail
- obstacle: the evidence points to an intruder inside, and the real answer is hidden in what is missing
- rule: none
- conflict: A, someone came out of the woods and is hiding in the house; B, nobody came in at all: the prints were made by someone leaving
- action: none

## Spine
depth: deep

### Constraints
- The first snow stopped at midnight. It starts again just after 2 a.m., and fresh snow will fill the tracks within minutes.
- Mum is on a night shift at the hospital. Her phone is in her locker, so calls go to voicemail.
- Only one pair of boots in the house has a star on the sole: yours, on the rack by the door.
- Walking backwards, people take short steps and press down on their toes; the toe of each print sinks deeper than the heel.
- The woods are forbidden: Mum's note on Ivy's door says NO WOODS, because there's a wild fox.
- Ivy is seven, clever and stubborn, and she's been reading a junior detective book.

### Alternatives
| # | central conflict | key decision | solution | ending |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | footprints enter and none leave: is an intruder hiding inside, or did someone leave and disguise it? | stop searching the house and follow the tracks into the storm | the missing boots and the deep toe prints show the tracks were made walking backwards, by someone leaving | you find your sister in the woods saving a fox; you both walk home forwards |
| 2 | LOST DOG posters on every lamppost, for a dog you've never seen, with your address as the owner's | take the dog in when it turns up at your door | the dog belonged to the house's previous family, who moved and left it behind | a dilemma: keep the dog, or drive it to a family who abandoned it |
| 3 | your new house's old baby monitor picks up a child counting down every night | knock on the neighbour's door instead of calling the police | the sound comes through the shared wall: a lonely boy is counting down to his dad's call from overseas | you and the boy count down together, and the call comes |
- chosen: 1. It gives the clearest physical evidence a viewer can solve (the boots), the strongest recontextualisation (every early image changes meaning), real time pressure (the snow), and a payoff that resolves the fear rather than adding a jump scare.

### Easy outs
| obstacle | obvious fix | why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| an intruder might be inside | call the police straight away | at first there's only an unlocked door and a thump with nobody found (E3, E4); once Ivy is gone, the trail is filling and can't wait (E9, E10). You leave a voicemail for Mum on the run |
| Ivy might be missing | check her room first | you did (E2); the pillows looked like her, which is the trick |
| finding her | wait for Mum | voicemail: Mum's phone is in her locker (constraint, E9) |
| the tracks | follow them any time | the snow starts again and fills them within minutes (E10) |
| why walk backwards at all | Ivy could just sneak out | she knew you'd follow her prints straight to the woods; reversed prints send you searching the house first, and the trick is in her detective book (E2, E9) |
