# Video 05: The Extra Door (Part 1), storyboard design (waiting for the owner's approval)

The approved script, narration, voice presets and line timings are unchanged. Every camera move, act, face, gesture,
glance, light, music mood and transition below already exists in the engine. New work is assets only (listed at the
end). What is behind the door is **never shown**: the door never opens, and the only sign of something there is two
shadows in the light under it.

**Length:** about 55 s (1650 frames). **Music:** the existing `mystery` mood (slow, dark, sparse minor pad), low under
the room tone. It drops out completely as the repeated "Let me in" ends, using the existing `musicDrop`. **Ambience:**
`amb_room` (room tone) and a clock ticking downstairs. **Light:** `interiorCool`, at night. A single warm pendant
lights the stairs end; the far end, with the old door, falls darker. It stays dark but always readable (the video QA
fails near-black frames).

## The set: Milo's upstairs hallway (new, built with the existing room builder)
- **Shape:** a corridor 1.6 m wide and about 7 m long, running away from the camera. The near end is the top of the
  stairs: the first steps going down on the left, with a banister and a newel post. This makes choice B's escape route
  visible from the first wide shot.
- **Walls:** four cream panel doors with brass knobs on the left wall and three on the right. Between them, framed
  pictures on nails, a wall clock, a runner rug and a small console table with a plant.
- **The old door:** centred on the far end wall. Dark, old, narrow wood, with no handle and no keyhole. It must look
  different from the cream doors at a glance: in colour, height, grain and the missing handle.
- **The picture clue** (owner notes: it must read instantly; nothing on the door should distract from it). The
  biggest picture in the hallway, a 1.2 × 0.8 m family photo, hung centred on the end wall. The old door has appeared
  through the middle of that spot:
  - **The clean patch.** On the end wall, a clearly visible rectangle of brighter, unfaded wallpaper (1.2 × 0.8 m,
    1.40–2.20 m high) with a faint dust line along its edges, where the frame protected the paper for years. The door
    is narrow (0.65 × 1.95 m), so the patch shows as one rectangle: a full-width band above the door, and wide strips
    (about 0.28 m) on both sides of the door frame.
  - **The nail.** On the wall directly above the patch, centred (about 2.25 m high), with nothing hanging from it.
  - **The photo.** The framed photo of Milo and Juno lies face-up on the floor, directly below, leaning against the
    door. It is visibly the same size as the clean patch, so the viewer matches the two at a glance.
  - **The door itself stays plain:** no scrap, no marks. All the attention goes to the patch, the nail and the photo.

  The clue is introduced in one **vertical framing** that the 9:16 frame suits: the nail at the top, the patch and
  the door in the middle, the photo at the bottom. Then one slow glide down to the photo, under Milo's line
  "...that's where our picture hangs." The patch is also faintly visible in the wide shot 2, for viewers who watch
  twice.

## Camera language and pacing
- **Shot types:** extreme close-ups, close-ups, over-the-shoulder shots, a point of view, macro inserts, one
  symmetrical wide, a reverse angle and a high wide.
- **Movement:** one whip (the energy of the count), glides (the count, the clue), one swoop (the stinger) and one
  slow push (the repeat). Hard cuts land on the knocks.
- **Variety:** no two consecutive shots use the same set-up, and there is no shot of Milo just standing and talking.
- **Pacing:** fast at the start (the hook and the count), then slower and quieter shots as the dread grows, with
  silence before every new piece of information. The biggest sound is a small click.

## Assets
**Reused, no changes:**
- Milo (his usual look) and his acts, faces, gestures and glances;
- every camera move and transition above;
- the `interiorCool` light;
- the `mystery` music mood and `musicDrop`;
- sounds: `amb_room`, `clock_tick`, `creak`, `crack`, `step_floor`;
- set dressing: the existing `pendant`, `picture`, `clock`, `rug`, `plant` and `chair` props;
- captions, sound mixing, rendering, QA, and the voice cache.

**New, all additions within the existing asset pipeline:**
1. **Set "hallway":** the corridor shell, using the room builder and the existing wallpaper and dado materials, plus
   the top of the stairs, the banister and the newel post.
2. **Prop "hallDoor":** a cream panel door with a brass knob (used seven times).
3. **Prop "oldDoor":** the old door, with stepped animation through the existing stepped-prop mechanism:
   - a shudder on each knock;
   - the light under the door breaking into two shadows;
   - the handle pushing out through the wood, then turning.

   It also carries the nail and the wallpaper scrap.
4. **Prop "photoFrame":** the photo of Milo and Juno, drawn simply in the house style.
5. **Prop "floatLabel":** a flat, unlit text card that floats in the scene (A, B). It reads as an on-screen label,
   not a physical sign. The engine has no anchored on-screen labels; its labels are diagram panels tied to a
   presenter.
6. **Item "phone"** in three states (typing, sent, reply), swapped on beats like the Video 04 apple.
7. **Four sounds:** `knock_heavy` (a single knock, so each lands on its own cut), `phone_buzz`, `phone_tap`,
   `latch_click`.

## Risks to check early in the asset stage (one still each, no full renders)
1. **The phone text is drawn as a held item.** It must stay sharp and readable in the macro insert. Fallback: draw
   the screen as a world-space display, like the readable score boards.
2. **The handle pushing through the wood** must read as unmistakable at preview resolution.
3. **Darkness against the QA's near-blank check:** dark, but always exposed enough to pass.
