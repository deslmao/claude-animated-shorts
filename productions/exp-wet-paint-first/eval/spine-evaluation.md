## Criteria
### clarity
- result: weak
- beats: E4, E5, E6, E9, E10
- evidence: E4 reasons "tacky at 7:30, so it was painted after the school opened at 6:45", then sets the goal "find out who was in before seven". E6 is "Thursday, 6:40", yet the sheet already shows entries for 6:47 and 6:55, and the school opens at 6:45. E5 paints the laughing figure with "a camera on an orange strap", but E9's photo is on "her phone". In E2 the threat is that "that corner gets painted over", and by then the corner holds only the intruder's picture.
- problem: A first-time viewer can follow who and where, but three things blur. First, the deduction: tacky paint at 7:30 means it was painted between 6:45 and about 7:30, so seven o'clock is an arbitrary cutoff. Second, which day's sheet Mira is reading at 6:40. Third, which device took the cruel photo. The stake is also unclear: painting over a corner that holds someone else's graffiti doesn't obviously hurt Mira's mural. Each of these makes the viewer stop to work something out that the plot doesn't need.
- fix: Make the deduction match its evidence. Either have Mira find the paint wet (not tacky) at 7:05, or have the sheet show that Asha is the only name signed before 7:30 on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday. Set E6 at 7:00 on Thursday, after Asha signs, or have Mira read the earlier days' sheets. Show in E1 or E2 that the corner is part of Mira's own composition (her signature, or the mural's central figure), so painting over it visibly ruins her entry.

After the E2 threat ("Your wall's next"), Leo's only role is to be cleared, twice (E3's brush strokes, E6's clean hands).

### emotional investment

## Stress tests
### plot hole
- applies: yes, it's a mystery with several obstacles
- result: fail
- beats: E1, E2, E3, E4, E5, E6, E10, E11
- finding: (1) **The obvious witness.** From 6:47 every morning a girl sits alone on the bench beside the wall, drawing (E1, and the rules). Mira passes her on Monday and never asks her what she saw until Thursday. The most-liked comment will be "She literally sits next to the wall every day, just ask her." (2) **The obvious stakeout.** After Monday, Mira could arrive at 6:45 instead of 7:30. She waits until Thursday (E6) without being shown a reason. (3) **The empty rule.** Okafor's "one more" is broken by the second and third pictures, and he doesn't act (E3, E5). Nobody at breakfast club notices a child painting the courtyard wall each morning beside the hall door either. (4) **The deduction.** The "before seven" cutoff doesn't follow from paint that's tacky at 7:30 (E4). (5) **The timeline.** Leo leaves "just before 7:00", but he's at the gate after Mira's 7:30 photographs (E2). (6) **The ending.** "Delete for everyone" on a two-week-old post isn't possible in common chat apps (E11), and it can't undo thirty-four views. Fixes: Mira asks Asha in E1 or E3, and Asha says "I didn't see anyone". That's a lie the sign-in sheet later exposes, which gives the viewer a checkable contradiction. Give Mira a reason to start early only on Thursday (the third picture shows the camera, so it's now personal). Re-time Okafor's warning and Leo's gate appearance. Replace the deletion with an apology made to the same chat.
### consistency
- applies: yes, the beats refer back to times, the rule and the corner's contents
- result: fail
- beats: E2, E3, E5, E6, E9, E10, E11
- finding: The packet contradicts itself in five places. (a) The "one more" rule (E2) against two further pictures with no consequence (E3, E5). (b) Leo "just before 7:00" (rules) against Leo at the gate after 7:30 (E2). (c) Thursday 6:40, before the 6:45 opening, against the 6:47 and 6:55 entries on the sheet (E6). (d) Three pictures in E10 against five in E11, with Mira painting only one. (e) The figure "lifts a camera on an orange strap" (E5) against the photo "on her phone" (E9). The table's other objects are consistent: the brush passes from Asha (E7) to Mira (E11), the roller stays unused, and the sketchbook stays with Asha.
### dilemma balance
- applies: yes, the writer frames a mystery between two explanations (A: Leo for revenge; B: someone telling a true story), and the viewer will weigh them
- result: weak
- beats: E1, E2, E3, E4, E6, E7
- finding: For A: Leo's threat, the spray tags, his suspension, and his presence at the hall door at 6:55 (E2, E6). For B: a girl matching the painted girl sits by the wall (E1); the brush strokes, not spray (E3); the panels form a narrative (E3, E5); the green smudge by Asha's name (E6). B wins decisively at E1 and E3, and A gains no evidence afterwards. That's acceptable when the real question is "why" and "who is the laughing figure", but Leo isn't a working red herring. Either give A a piece of evidence that cuts both ways (Leo had green spray paint at the bike sheds; Leo's brother is in the laughing group) or treat Leo as background and spend the seconds on the realisation.
