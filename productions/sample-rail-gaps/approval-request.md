# The Engineer Who Left Gaps on Purpose: story approval

**Format:** action-first. The opening action (leaving gaps in a railway deliberately) looks like incompetence, so curiosity comes first; the mechanism (thermal expansion) is explained through a visible summer failure, then a twist on how modern track solves it.

**Estimated length:** 55 s · **Words:** 157 · **Narration:** 886 characters (about 886 ElevenLabs credits) · **Voices:** narrator=george, milo=liam, gus=brian

## Premise
- **question:** Why would an engineer deliberately leave gaps in a railway, and why don't modern railways need them?
- **protagonist:** Gus, a track engineer (fictional everyman)
- **want:** a railway that survives summer
- **fear:** the track bending under a train
- **protects:** the passengers
- **decision:** leave gaps despite the mockery
- **action:** Gus leaves a finger-wide gap between every rail, on purpose, while a rival crew lays a perfectly smooth track
