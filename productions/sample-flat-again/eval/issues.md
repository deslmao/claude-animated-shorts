# Phase-1 issues: Flat Again
Each must appear in story.md's ## Issue log with a resolution and a status (resolved / no longer applies: reason).

- **I1** (clarity, weak): Grandad's key gesture sits between two finger-counts, so a first-time viewer will most likely read his "two fingers" as a count ("two lates?") and not as a squeeze on a brake lever. The clue that is meant to carry the payoff gets misfiled. E3 is the densest beat in the film and holds three of the four clues, so some of them will be lost on a first watch. "Since Sunday" points at something the viewer never saw.
  - suggested fix: Keep the two kinds of fingers apart. Have Mr. Shah count lates as tally marks (chalk on the shop door), or have Grandad's gesture be unmistakably a grip: his good hand closing round an imaginary handlebar, then squeezing. Split E3 into the padlock and keys on one side and Grandad's attempt on the other (the dinner table, a moment later). Change "since Sunday" to an event the viewer sees (see consistency).
- **I2** (emotional investment, weak): The ending ("he was protecting me, and I ignored him") lands only if we already care about Grandad and about what was lost between you. As planned, the viewer meets him as an obstacle and a suspect, and the warmth arrives only after the reveal. Why a thirteen-year-old cares so much about this round is never shown, so the first half's stakes feel generic.
  - suggested fix: Add one pre-stroke image of the bond in E1, under the torch, for example the bike's frame carrying a RAY'S CYCLES sticker (he built it for you), or a photo on the shed wall of him holding the seat of your first bike, tapping two fingers on your helmet. E13's tap then becomes a returned ritual, not a new one. Give the round one concrete want, such as a coin jar or a savings card with a goal on it, seen once in E1 or E3.
- **I3** (plot hole, weak): "He can't write" and "his right hand can't grip" pre-empt the note and the do-it-yourself repair. But E9 shows the means he had all along: take your hand, put it on the lever and squeeze. He can walk (slowly, with a stick), and he can reach the shed (E7, E8), so nothing stopped him from doing on Monday evening what he does on Wednesday. In E3 he only points from the table, and Mum (who is present and an adult) is never physically shown the problem. The top-comment probe is "He could've just walked you to the shed and squeezed the brake on day one." Smaller probes: why the spare key is hung right by Grandad's door (convenient), and why you shrug off Monday's near-miss at the junction without checking the brake. The pump in his pocket on Wednesday (E8) is acceptable, since he is keeping it on him so you can't find it, but the viewer gets no hint of that. Fix: turn the hole into the emotional cost. In E3, show Grandad actually try. He gets up, takes your wrist and tugs you toward the shed. You pull away and put your earbuds in, and Mum steers him back to his chair ("Sit down, Dad, it's locked now"). Then nobody can say he didn't try the direct route. Hang the spare on the hook by the back door (which happens to be beside his room) so its position reads as household routine. In E2, give the protagonist a reason not to look at the brake: the clock, and Mr. Shah already counting.
- **I4** (consistency, weak): (1) The Wednesday timeline doesn't fit Monday's. On Monday you find the tyres at 5:40, pump them and still reach the shop at 6:12, so the trip takes about 25 minutes. On Tuesday the round on foot starts at 6:25. On Wednesday Grandad takes the key at 5:20, and then come the confrontation, the demonstration, the realisation, fitting a new cable with a stroke-limited guide, and pumping two tyres. You still "ride down Gallows Hill" at 5:58 and are at the shop at 6:00. Either the ride takes two minutes (so Monday's 12-minute lateness makes no sense) or Wednesday is impossible. (2) "Since Sunday" (E10) refers to an event that is never shown, and the viewer never sees how or when Grandad found the fray. (3) Object continuity mostly holds. The spare key goes from the hook (E3, E5, E6) to the tartan hand (E7) to the padlock (E8), and the pump goes from the hook (E1, E2) to missing (E4) to his pocket (E8) to your hands (E11). The caps appear on the bench at E8 but have no E8 row in the objects table, the earbuds' presence in the shed at E10 is not tracked, and the helmet (E13) is never established. Fix: move Grandad's arrival to about 4:50, after Mum's 4:30 car, which keeps her alibi, and have the clock read about 5:35 as you wheel the bike out, so the ride matches Monday's. Either show the discovery (Sunday evening, Grandad in the shed doorway, his eyes on the lever as you squeeze it to park) or change the reference to Monday. Add the E8 caps row and the earbuds and helmet to the table.
- **I5** (dilemma balance, weak): The evidence at the final beat before the reveal (E7) is lopsided. Against Dex there is only a grin and "Enjoy it while it lasts" (E2), and he is ruled out at E5 by checkable logic (the locked shed, a key only someone in the house can reach). That is good, but it removes him early. Against Mum there is one spoken motive line (E3), the fact that she fitted the lock, and the fact that she is up before 5:40. She is cleared at E6 by checkable evidence (the car leaves and the key stays), which is a strong, fair beat. Against Grandad there are the caps that rhyme with his pill pots, the gestures and the pointing, the hook by his door, a mechanic's skill and the tartan gown. Grandad clearly wins for anyone paying attention by E3 to E5. Because the real mystery is why, an obvious who is tolerable, but the stakeout then has little doubt to resolve. Fix: give Mum one piece of physical evidence that genuinely fits both readings, shown in E4 or E5. For example, show that her 4:30 route takes her past the hook and the shed, or show her quietly resetting your alarm "so you can sleep". The E6 alibi then removes a real suspect.
- **I6** (cold viewer, weak): A first-time viewer would think: "Someone kept letting my bike tyres down so I'd lose my paper round, so I locked the shed, staked out the spare key, and caught Grandad doing it. He couldn't talk, so he showed me the brake cable was about to snap on the hill; he'd been protecting me, and we fixed it together and I made it on time." The likely misreadings are these. The curling fingers will be read as a count, not a squeeze, until E9. Some viewers will think the brake already half-failed on Monday's near-miss. The ending raises "why didn't he just show me on Monday?" and "how did I fix it and get there in two minutes?". Viewers will also wonder where Sunday came from.
