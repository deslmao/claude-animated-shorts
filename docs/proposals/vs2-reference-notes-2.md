# Reference clip 2: camera language (technique only)

**Source:** the screen recording sent on 6 Nov 2026 (16.5 s, 832 × 1498, 60 fps, no audio), an excerpt of a Rick's Clips
railway Short. No frames are committed, and none of its characters, scenes or script are reused.
**Method:** contact sheet at 4 frames / s, and the mean frame-to-frame change at 30 fps, to separate cuts from camera
moves.

## What it does
- **Zero hard cuts in 16.5 s.** Every change of place is a continuous camera move: flights, a pull-out, a fly-in
  through a window and back out, and a short dissolve.
- **The camera is almost always moving.** The mean frame change is 10-20 for most half-seconds. It goes nearly still
  (about 1.6) only for a punchline beat ("without a sound"), so stillness is used as emphasis.
- **Rhythm:** a new framing about every 1-1.5 s, often on the key word of the caption.
- **Low, subject-level angles:** the camera at rail height, looking up at a character, makes ordinary objects
  dramatic.
- **Fly-through transitions:** the camera pushes into a window to enter a new space, then pulls back out to the
  exterior. Space is always readable: you know where you are.
- **Object inserts as explanation:** a newspaper headline is pushed in to an extreme close-up, then the camera whips
  out to the crowd.
- **Reaction beats:** a medium close-up on a character in a window, holding still while the expression changes word
  by word.
- **Sharp landings:** blur happens only while the camera travels. Every frame the camera lands on is crisp, which is
  exactly what our transition-blur fix targets.

## How Thinkora translates it (Video 03)
- Continuous flights and fly-throughs between rooms, through the doorway itself, instead of cuts.
- A low angle at floor or keys level, and point-of-view shots through the door.
- An object insert on the keys: the keys carry the memory.
- Holds on the punchline beats only.
- A new framing on the key word of each line.
- Controlled blur: motion while travelling, sharp on arrival.
